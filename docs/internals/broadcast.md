# Internals: broadcast, queue and speech timing

Why the queue, transition and speech-timing code is shaped the way it is. The
short invariants are in the root `CLAUDE.md`; read the matching section here
before changing any of them, because most of these paragraphs record a measured
failure and the fix that was chosen over the obvious one.

Covers: the drain/pair-drain path, album/block queueing, stem blends, operator
skips, spoken-clock forecasting, air-time stamping, the listener buffer, the listener-count gates,
TTS engine routing, personas, guests, programmes and the policy switches
(voice, clock, token budget).

- **Queue/playback path**: `queue.drainToLiquidsoap()` is the single writer of `next.txt` (+ request-intro `say.txt`); `queue.playJingle()` is the single writer of `jingle-now.txt`; `queue.announce()` is the single writer of scheduled `say.txt`/`intro.txt`. Request-intro WAVs are written ~250ms before the track URI so the 0.5s voice poll picks them up first. Track handoffs poll at 1.0s; voice and manual-jingle handoffs at 0.5s. The manual jingle has a dedicated `request.queue` wrapped around both `dj_queue` and the automatic rotate, so it wins the next **safe** boundary even when a track or automatic jingle is already queued; `source.available` leaves it queued while voice or a bed is live. **Pair-aware drains** (`transitions.pairDrain`, default on, **DJ-mode personas only** — `queue.pairDrainActive()`, `broadcast/drain-policy.ts`): picks are HELD unsent until their successor is queued behind them (the deadline routine in the watcher tick picks it ~120s before the on-air track's effective end, `min(duration, cue_out)`, with a failure-retry cooldown of `DEADLINE_PICK_COOLDOWN_SEC`; hard fallback ~45s drains with intrinsic stamps), which is what lets `applyPairStamps` finally apply the pair-sized `crossSecondsFor` (#749) and lets stem blends insert. A successor of ANY kind releases the hold (a request IS the successor arriving — FIFO is never inverted); unknown clock (boot/recover/untracked auto) or a non-DJ persona drains eagerly, byte-for-byte the old behaviour. **Operator skips commit first (#1300 bug 6)**: because the hold makes an empty `dj_queue` the NORMAL mid-track state, `POST /dj/skip` runs `queue.commitBeforeSkip()` — force-drain plus a bounded wait for the `dj_queue_status` telnet probe to report a RESOLVED request (a sent-but-still-fetching request loses the fallback race just the same) — before the telnet `skip`; on timeout the skip proceeds anyway and the response/booth log say the pick wasn't confirmed, so `auto.m3u` filling the slot is reported, never silent. Policy in `broadcast/skip-policy.ts`, pinned by `scripts/skip-policy.test.ts`; an empty queue keeps the bare-skip path byte-for-byte. **Stem blends** (`transitions.stemBlends`, opt-in; `broadcast/stem-blend.ts` + worker `render_transition`): at a pair drain, a cache-hit-only render mixes the outgoing tail stems × incoming head stems (`state/stems/`, written during analysis when `audio.stemCache` is on) into a clip that airs between them — outgoing cues out at the blend start, the clip carries the INCOMING track's identity (now-playing flips at blend start; `lastSeenKey` dedups the real fire; `subwave_clip="1"` keeps telnet rid helpers honest), incoming cues in past its pre-mixed head. Any miss → plain pair-aware crossfade. **A clip carries no `liq_amplify`** (`subsonic.getClipUri`), so its LEVEL is whatever the render baked in: both sides are gained by `music/loudness.ts` `resolveGainDb` — the same figure the drain stamps on a real track — never by re-deriving from the analyzer's leading-window LUFS, which ignores ReplayGain tags (the default source), the boost cap and the peak headroom cap and is what made rendered seams sit at a different level than the tracks around them (#1240). The render's peak guard ducks the BORROWED loop to fit the ceiling rather than scaling the whole clip, because scaling the clip is scaling the incoming track's own content back out of match. Pinned by `scripts/stem-blend-level.test.ts`. Clips sweep after 1h (clips still queued for an unaired seam are exempt — `queue.pendingClipPaths()`); the stem cache is byte-budget LRU (`audio.stemCacheGb`, 1–1000 GB, edited in Settings → Transitions), and tail stems carry a `tail-meta.json` alignment sidecar (exact decoded tail offset — the render treats stems without it as a miss). **The stem cache backfills** like the CLAP/vocal scopes: `tracks.stems_at` (schema v17) stamps every stem ATTEMPT, `db.needsStemsIds()` is the fourth widening in `runAnalysisPass`, so turning the cache on for an already-analysed library fills in over successive resumable passes instead of needing a `--re-analyze` (which wipes every vector and can't resume). The stamp records the attempt, **not disk presence** — the LRU sweep evicts dirs by design, so a presence-based scope would re-separate every evicted track forever on any library bigger than the budget; a miss at seam time just falls back to a plain crossfade. **Which tracks get stems IS the feature (#1622 FR 14)**: the budget always binds on a real library — a 15 GB cache holds ~1.1k tracks of a 55k catalogue — and `needsStemsIds` used to be `ORDER BY id` over Navidrome's opaque hashes, i.e. chance. The ranking lives in `music/stem-priority.ts` (pure) and is projected into SQL by `library-db/stem-scan.ts`, with `scripts/stem-priority.test.ts` scoring every crafted row BOTH ways and demanding the same integer — the era filter's rule, because two implementations of one scorer drift silently and nothing says so. Its shape is a **multiplier, not a tier**: the bar grids `maybeRenderBlend` demands (`inn.bars` for the incoming side, `out.outro.bars` + `out.durationSec` for the outgoing one) count the seam sides a track can serve (0/1/2) and MULTIPLY a value sum of recent airplay, an ever-aired floor and curation (an operator heart outranks a listener like, per the station-wide rule). So a track with no grid at all is worth zero however loved — its stems are bytes the only consumer is guaranteed to reject — while a one-sided track with a heart still outranks a two-sided track nothing has ever played; a tier that merely added would have got the first wrong, a tier that merely gated would have sealed the second. **Nothing is starved**: the untouched majority of a library ties EXACTLY, and the tie breaks on `RANDOM()` (as `deepCutTracks` samples) rather than on id, because a frozen order over a scope larger than the budget loses the same tail every night — forever. Resumption never depended on the order anyway; the `stems_at` stamp is what a resumed pass reads, which is exactly what frees the order to be a ranking. Likes are INJECTED (`stemCache.likeSignals()` at the call site) rather than read in `library-db`, and an absent store — the tagger CLI never loads `state/likes.json` — just drops the curation term. **The sweep had to change with it, and is not a refinement.** The scan writes the BEST tracks first, so they carry the OLDEST mtimes: kept oldest-out, the byte-budget LRU would have deleted precisely what the ranking earned, and `stems_at` stamps the attempt so those tracks would never be separated again. `stemEvictionOrder` sorts lowest-priority-first with mtime as the tiebreak, which keeps "a re-analysis refreshes a dir's slot" true inside every tie — and is the whole sort when the priority lookup fails (a closed library DB), so the fallback is byte-for-byte the pre-#1622 sweep. A dir whose track is no longer in the catalogue evicts before anything real. **The budget gates every stem write, not just the widening (#1257)**: `runAnalysisPass` takes ONE `headroomTracks()` figure per pass and spends it per NET-NEW dir via the pure `stemCache.stemWriteDecision` (a rewrite of an existing dir is free) — ride-along stems used to bypass the cap entirely, which is how a vocal backfill grew a 500 GB budget to 674 GB. Headroom is sized off the cache's own measured average once it holds ≥50 dirs (`estimateTrackBytes`; the fixed 25 MB guess ran ~2x pessimistic in the field), and `sweep()` reports `failedDirs`/`overBudgetBytes` so a sweep that can't evict (permissions on a relocated stems mount) is loud in the booth log, the analyze event log and the doctor instead of returning `{removed: 0}` and reading as "nothing to do". The admin queue header names only the imminent transition after its incoming item is sent (before drain it shows `—`, never a raw proposal), while a `STEM BLEND` text badge stays on the successor row that actually carries `stemSeam`; both are stamped at pair drain, cleared on cancel and definitive rather than predictive. Pinned by `scripts/stem-backfill.test.ts` + `scripts/stem-cache-sweep.test.ts`; the header's label (both sides of the pair, and the sent gate that keeps it a fact rather than a forecast) by `scripts/queue-transition-label.test.ts`.
- **Album / block queueing (#1622 FR 4, Discord ask in #1568)**: `POST /dj/queue-block` puts a whole album — or a run of an artist's tracks — on air as ONE operator press, and `DELETE /dj/queue/block/:blockId` takes the rest off as one. **It is not a bypass feature, and the tracker framed it as one.** Every guard named there was checked: `picker.albumHours` (`filterPickerCandidates`, `dj-agent/album-guard.ts`) and the artist guard (`dj-agent/artist-guard.ts`) are PICK paths, and an operator push reaches neither; what the cooldown does to a queued block is already right, since `queue.recentAlbumKeys` walks `upcoming` and so stops the picker adding more of the same record behind it. The exemptions a block needs are the two `POST /dj/queue-track` already carried and it carries them unchanged — `allowDuplicate: true` past the #619 dedup guard (which is also why a block may hold one track twice, so the cancel addresses ITEMS and never re-resolves by id), and `requestedBy: 'studio'` past the #447 cap, the boundary cut and the bed's request reason. Nothing new reaches the drain: **`QueueItem.block` is identity only** (the badge, one booth line instead of thirty, the block cancel), and a third air-path discriminator there is exactly how "in a block" would become a fourth kind of track. **The never-play list is NOT bypassed** — absolute, requests included: `planBlock` reads `blocklist.hitOf` so the response can name each refusal, but `queue.push()` is still the gate and a `-2` is recorded as a skip whether or not the plan predicted it; blocked tracks are also removed BEFORE the cap, so two never-play cuts on a long record do not cost two MORE off the end. Four decisions pull against each other and all four are deliberate. An album keeps its own **disc/track order** and is REFUSED `shuffle` and `limit` rather than having them quietly ignored — a shuffled album is not a smaller version of the request, it is the twelve-tracks-by-one-artist an operator could already queue by hand. The 30-track cap **truncates and reports**: refusing a double album over its length is the wrong answer to an explicit action, and dropping six tracks silently is worse than either. Pair-drain is left alone — items 1..N-1 each have a successor and drain `send-pair` in one pass (not a new shape: `requests.maxPending` already allows six stacked), the tail holds to its own deadline near the end of the block, and `maybeDeadlinePick` correctly stands down while the head is sent; special-casing the drain for a block would be the "second rule at a pick path" mistake in a different costume, and the honest lever is the cap. And a block that outlasts the current show is **warned about (`runsPastShowChange`), never cut** — cutting contradicts "an explicit operator action always fires", while a mic-pass between two tracks of one record is the worse outcome. That warning is really about the handover: `runPickCycle` (which airs the mic-pass) only fires when `upcoming` is EMPTY, so a block spanning a boundary holds a pending handoff until it drains and a long enough one outlives `HANDOFF_MAX_AGE_MS`. The span it is measured from is the PLAYABLE one (`music/silence-trim.ts`) from the block's expected START, the same two rules `resolveBoundaryCut` follows, and one unmeasured track makes the whole total null rather than short — a short total under-states the overrun, which is the direction that reads as "this fits" when it does not. **A listener request landing behind a block is told how long**: FIFO is not negotiable (`drain-policy.ts` states that a request IS the successor arriving and that FIFO is never inverted, and jumping the block would split the record the feature exists to keep whole), so the three sites that queue a request append `requestWaitClause` — which NAMES the block and gives a rounded number, and says nothing at all when the forecast is unknown or under five minutes, i.e. the pre-block behaviour. Its forecast is `queue.airForecastSec`, deliberately NOT `remainingUntilItemAirs`: that one walks only the SENT chain, which is right for the drain (nothing unsent is ever ahead of the item it asks about) and wrong for a listener with three unsent album tracks in front of them. **It counts the BED**, like every other clock that walks this queue — `maybePushBed` writes straight to `next.txt` so a bed is never an `upcoming` entry, and it reuses `bedDelayBeforeItemAirs` rather than re-walking (that helper sums the item's own bed plus the beds of SENT items ahead; an UNSENT item ahead legitimately contributes zero, since its bed is decided at ITS drain). Both callers are understated by a miss in the direction that matters — the wait notice says a request is closer than it is, on the one surface it exists to make honest, and `runsPastShowChange` under-reports the overrun, which reads as "this fits" when it does not. Pinned by `scripts/queue-block.test.ts` and `scripts/queue-block-wiring.test.ts`.
- **Show-boundary fade (#1574)**: a schedule built on long-form material (ambient, classical, prog) picks its last track minutes before the slot ends and is still playing it deep into the next show — so the incoming presenter's opening link airs over the outgoing show's music, which is the one thing a schedule grid is supposed to make impossible. `broadcast/show-boundary.ts` owns the whole policy; `queue.resolveBoundaryCut` is a thin caller. **Off by default at both levels** (`settings.fadeAtShowEnd`, and a show's own tri-state `fadeAtShowEnd` where `null` = inherit) so an upgrade sounds byte-identical — and the show field is tri-state rather than a plain boolean precisely because a boolean would read every existing show as an explicit "no" the moment an operator turned the station default on. **The cut is not a new mechanism**: it folds into the SAME `liq_cue_out` the #447 length cap and the silence trim already write, arbitrated "earliest wins" by `subsonic.getAnnotatedUri`, because those three all cut the same tail and only stay consistent while one call site folds them together. Four things are easy to get wrong. (1) The boundary is computed from the pick's **expected air time**, never from `now` — the drain's clock is a forecast (the paired guards in `broadcast/queue/pure.ts` are the same rule), and a track held in `dj_queue` would otherwise be cut short by however long it waits. (2) The scan walks the **station** clock: schedule slots fire at the hours the operator painted them in (#353) and zones sit at :30/:45 offsets, so `stationHourBoundaries` steps minute by minute rather than adding an hour to an instant, and a timed takeover's start/expiry (#930) rides in as an extra candidate because it is not hour-aligned. (3) The span measured is the **playable** one — after the cap and the trimmed tail, through `drain-policy.playableDurationSec` and `music/silence-trim.ts`, never the tagged duration and never a local subtraction — and the returned cue is an ABSOLUTE offset, so a head-trimmed track's cut moves by exactly the skipped seconds. (4) Two floors pull in opposite directions and both matter: `BOUNDARY_TOLERANCE_SEC` (60) leaves a small overrun alone, because the incoming show's first pick has its own latency and an early ending costs more than a minute of overhang; `BOUNDARY_MIN_PLAY_SEC` (90) refuses to cut a track down to a stub, because a record that starts and stops is worse than the spill. **Their sum is the feature's own floor**, and it is silent: a track needs more than 150 playable seconds to be cut, so a `maxTrackSeconds` cap at or below that leaves boundary fading inert for tracks using that cap. The cap limits duration, but does not guarantee no spill: a 120-second track starting 30 seconds before changeover still overruns by 90 seconds because the minimum-play guard refuses the cut. Shows can override the station cap. The admin hint explains this using the served `values.boundaryFadeMinTrackSeconds` rather than a number restated in the web build (#1591). Listener requests are exempt, mirroring the #447 cap — an explicit ask plays in full. A rendered stem blend is vetoed by an armed cut through the existing `outCapped` flag, for the reason the cap vetoes it: the clip is mixed from a tail that will not air. **The mixer is told WHY the track stops** (`liq_show_fade`, read off the OUTGOING track in `radio.liq`'s `dj_transition`): the washout and the loop are gestures built around the track's own ending, and the loop branch applies no fader at all — measured on a reduced harness that mirrors the arming and fade-shape selection, a loop-armed track cut at a boundary held full level right through the cross and stopped dead (-33.6 dB at the seam, i.e. the two tracks summing), where the same cut with `liq_show_fade` restored the equal-amplitude curve (-39.1 dB at the midpoint) and walked the outgoing out. **A boundary cut is a plain crossfade, and that is enforced in two places on purpose.** The mixer disarms all six gestures, the incoming-side four included — they are not garnish on `b`, every one of them reshapes `a_source`, and forcing `washing`/`looping` false is precisely what would arm them on seams where the outgoing gesture used to suppress them. The DRAIN strips the outgoing ones upstream too (the same five deletes a rendered blend does, and for the same reason), because the flag is the only thing carrying the precedence: a controller running ahead of its broadcast image would otherwise hand an armed loop to a mixer that has never heard of `liq_show_fade`, which is the hard stop the feature exists to prevent. The strip is safe against the cap's own auto-armed washout because an armed boundary cut is always at least `BOUNDARY_TOLERANCE_SEC` EARLIER than the capped end — the overshoot test is what arms it — so the ending being stripped is never the cap's; and it happens BEFORE `applyPairStamps`, which bails out on an armed washout or loop and would otherwise leave `crossSec` sized for the wind-down of an outro that no longer airs. **One more thing the forecast has to count: the BED.** `maybePushBed` writes a bed straight to `next.txt`, so it is never an `upcoming` entry and `remainingUntilItemAirs` walks straight past it; left uncounted the cut lands a whole link late and the track spills exactly what the feature exists to stop, so `maybePushBed` records `bedDelaySec` (the bed's length less the two crosses it OVERLAPS — the predecessor's exit canvas in, its own out) and `bedDelayBeforeItemAirs` folds it back in. Pinned by `scripts/show-boundary.test.ts` (the pure policy) and `scripts/show-boundary-drain.test.ts` (the exemptions, the bed, the earlier-than-the-cap invariant and the plain-crossfade rule).
- **Takeover "until the schedule changes" (#1601)**: the timed takeover (#930) only ever offered fixed durations, so an operator pinning a show mid-slot had to guess how long the slot had left and then either re-pin or over-pin and shadow the next scheduled show. `broadcast/takeover-window.ts` resolves `expiresAt` from the next weekly-grid boundary instead of `now + N`. **Nothing new is stored**: `ScheduleOverride.expiresAt` has always been an absolute instant rather than a duration, so the choice is spent at `POST /schedule/override` and never persisted — `resolveActiveShow`, the janitor sweep, the programme span and the roster sweep all keep reading an ordinary pin and cannot tell which way the window was picked. `until` rides on the REQUEST (`schemas/schedule.ts`, default `'fixed'`, so a body written before this is byte-identical), and `minutes` is required under `'fixed'` and refused under `'schedule-change'` — both halves of one rule, that the two fields must not be able to disagree about what was asked for. Demanding a duration the server then ignores is one way to let them; silently discarding one the caller did send is the worse way, since nothing tells the caller its number went nowhere. Four things are easy to get wrong. (1) The boundary is the **grid's**, not the resolver's: `resolveActiveShow` honours a live takeover, so scanning through it answers "when does the pin I am replacing run out", which is nobody's question — the scan hands that same resolver a snapshot with the override taken out rather than restating the day/hour lookup, because a second copy is how re-pinning during a takeover comes to disagree with the grid it is measured against. (2) The scan is `show-boundary.ts`'s `nextShowChangeMs` reused, with no `extra` candidates: it walks the STATION clock minute by minute (slots fire at the hours the operator painted them in, #353, and zones sit at :30/:45), and a takeover's own start/expiry are exactly what this question excludes. (3) **There is a CEILING and NO FLOOR, and the asymmetry is the whole rule** — the same one the track-length cap and floor are held apart by. A ceiling TRIMS a window that is otherwise valid, so a grid with no change inside the horizon still produces a bounded pin (`schemas/schedule.ts` keeps `OVERRIDE_MAX_MINUTES` so a forgotten pin cannot shadow the grid for days). A floor cannot LENGTHEN a window that is genuinely short — it can only replace the request with a different one, and here that different one is the bug: `OVERRIDE_MIN_MINUTES` was applied on this path as the ceiling's mirror image, so a boundary five minutes out stored a fifteen-minute pin that ran ten minutes PAST the change, shadowing the incoming show, which is the single harm the option exists to remove. It no longer applies to a resolved instant; it still bounds `until: 'fixed'`, where it is a limit on what an operator may type. `scheduleOverrideSchema` imposes no minimum of its own (only `expiresAt > startedAt` and the cap), so a five-minute pin is an ordinary stored override — which is why the floor was never needed for storability, and why the test that checks every resolved window is storable had to gain a second assertion (`expiresAt <= nextChangeAt`) to pin the rule at all. **The trade is accepted, not overlooked**: the switch lands at the next track boundary, so a window shorter than the track now on air may air the takeover late, or lapse having aired nothing, costing a session roll each way. A pin asked to end at the change ending early is a disappointment; one that runs past the change is the bug. (4) The window is **explained, not just given**: `GET /schedule/next-change` serves the resolved end plus the `source` that decided it — `schedule` (the grid's own change, however near), `maximum` (no change in reach, held to the longest pin the station allows) and `ceiling` (a change supplied past that limit, trimmed to it; unreachable from the live scan, whose horizon IS the ceiling, and kept distinct because "the grid never moves on" and "it moves on, later than a takeover can run" are opposite news). The dialog shows the concrete time and the reason before the operator commits, and the booth log prints the same reason. The dialog's failure states are checked BEFORE its data: TanStack Query keeps `data` across both a failed refetch and a disabled query, so reading it first let a dead controller be answered with the last end time it happened to know, and a re-entry to the option repaint a cached absolute instant already in the past — the card requires the data to be newer than the current selection. It is offered for a Default programming takeover (`showId: null`) on the same terms as a show pin: "drop the scheduled show and coast until the grid moves on" is the same gesture, and the boundary belongs to the grid rather than to whatever is pinned over it. The preview is deliberately NOT folded into the public `GET /schedule` the dialog already polls — the scan is a minute at a time across twelve hours, and that is not a cost to hand every listener-facing schedule read; it is advisory either way, since the pin resolves the window again at its own `startedAt`. Pinned by `scripts/takeover-window.test.ts`.
- **Spoken clocks on pick-attached links (#864 → #1282 → #1314)**: a link is written when the pick is MADE but airs when the pick STARTS, so any time it speaks is a **forecast** — `linkAirDate(showAt)`, i.e. now plus whatever is left of the on-air track. The forecast holds only if the pick reaches Liquidsoap before that track ends; when the round outruns it, `dj_queue` is empty at the seam, `auto.m3u` fills the slot and the pick airs at the END of that filler, with the link still naming the moment the filler STARTED (#1314's ~4-minute undershoot). #1282's removal of a fixed +120s show-attribution overshoot is what exposed it — the two errors had been cancelling. **Two guards, both in `broadcast/queue/pure.ts`, and they must stay paired**: `linkClockAt(showAt, now)` refuses to hand the generator a clock at all below `LINK_CLOCK_MIN_RUNWAY_SEC` (= `DRAIN_DEADLINE_SEC` — `maybeDeadlinePick`'s empty-queue backstop always fires inside that window on any station, whatever its track lengths; measured over 788 rounds, 27.7% ran past 45s of runway but only 0.3% past 120s; accepted flip side: a sub-2-minute-track library never clears the floor even at track start, so its links never speak the clock at all), and `linkClockDrifted` drops the line at `airIntro` when the real seam lands more than `LINK_CLOCK_DRIFT_TOLERANCE_SEC` (90s) from the stamped `item.linkClockAt`. Runway is read from the LIVE clock at generation, never from the `leadSec` `showAt` was built with — on the pool path the pick call has already spent part of it. The stamp records the clock being **offered**, not used: unlike `shouldDropStaleLink`'s `mentionsTrack`, a clock has no reliable string handle ("18:38" / "twenty to seven" / "eighteen thirty-eight"), so don't narrow it with a regex. Note that even a perfect forecast is heard `stream.bufferSeconds` late — see the next bullet.
- **The hourly check's WORDING varies, its reading does not (#1602)**: `spokenTimePhrase` bought #1282's accuracy by rounding the wall clock into six fixed minute bands and handing the prompt one string with "say exactly that time … never a different time" — and since the check rides the `:00` cron it lands in the first band almost every time, so every hour of every day opened with the identical five words. The model was being obedient, not repetitive. Each band in `time.ts`'s `TIME_BANDS` now carries SEVERAL wordings of the one rounded time (`spokenTimePhrases`, canonical form first; `spokenTimePhrase` still returns that first form, so every other caller is byte-identical), the band rides on `context.clock.spokenTimeOptions`, and `nextHourlyTimeClause` (`llm/internal/prompts/scripts.ts`) picks ONE through the no-repeat picker that already keeps narrative angles from settling (`pickTimePhrase`, `prompts/context.ts`) before dictating it in the unchanged clause — `next`, not `build`, because picking ADVANCES that rotation, so the function is not the pure formatter its old name implied and must keep exactly one production caller. **The prompt is never shown the band**: offering the model a list is the latitude #1282 removed, and a model handed options leans on the first one anyway — which is the repetition being fixed. Two rules bind anything added to a band. Every form must be interchangeable at **every** minute in that band **including the minute the band opens on**, not just at the one you had in mind — which is why the `:25`–`:39` band cannot say "gone half past", the `:15`–`:24` band cannot say "gone quarter past", the `:00`–`:04` band cannot say "a minute or so past" (that band opens on the very minute the cron fires), and no band may drop its qualifier for a bare hour. The broadcast buffer is not a defence for a form that overclaims: a listener does hear this `stream.bufferSeconds` late, but that is an operator dial in another module, so a wording whose honesty depends on it stops being honest when the dial moves. The safe direction is to widen ("around quarter past"), never to sharpen. The hour word is always `spokenHourPhrase`'s, never re-derived, or the day-edge normalisation (23:50 → "coming up on midnight") goes with it. Pinned by `scripts/clock-phrase.test.ts` (every wording in a band names the same hour as the canonical one, at all 1440 minutes; three-form floor per band, since the no-repeat picker turns a two-form band into a deterministic alternation) and `scripts/hourly-time-phrasing.test.ts` (the clause quotes exactly one wording, always from the current band, never the same one twice running, and all three legacy fallback clauses byte for byte). The per-band form lists are a SNAPSHOT so a change reads as a deliberate diff — the interchangeability rule itself is not mechanically checkable and stays a review-time human check; only the hour word is machine-checked. `clock.spokenTimeOptions` is controller-internal and `routes/public.ts` strips it from `GET /now-playing`: the band is the picker's raw material, not a station fact, and a public read never widens to carry a behaviour internal.
- **Vocal-aware timing for scheduled segments (#1622 FR 5a)**: `enforceIntroBudget` — the never-talk-over-a-singer rule — was applied in exactly one place, `broadcast/dj-agent/enqueue.ts`'s `trimLinkToIntro` on the pick's link. Every other spoken segment (station idents, the hourly check, weather, skill segments) reached the air with no budget at all, and since #1485 FR 5b `djTalkOnlyBetweenTracks` routes all of them through `holdForNextTrack` → `airPendingVoice`, i.e. onto the HEAD of the incoming track on the light-duck intro channel — the *same* runway the link is trimmed against. **The lever there is placement, not text, and that is forced by the order things happen in.** The link is written for a known song and trimmed before the TTS runs; a scheduled segment is rendered into a WAV before the boundary it will take is known (`announce()` renders and only then reads the talk-air scope; `announceAtNextTrack` renders NOW precisely so TTS latency stays off the air path) and `airPendingVoice` may hold the result across several boundaries afterwards, so by the time the incoming track is a fact there is no text left to trim. A clip that would outlast the runway therefore does what a segment already does when the boundary is busy (`boundaryCarriesTrackVoice`): it keeps its slot and takes the next one. Postpone, never cancel; nothing is regenerated, and the wait is bounded by the `PENDING_VOICE_MAX_AGE_MS` staleness check that was already at the top of that method rather than by a new timeout. `broadcast/vocal-runway.ts` owns the rule and the measurement. **The measurement is composed in exactly one place now**, which is the other half of the change: `bed-policy.rampBudgetMs` (the three-state read of `vocalRanges` — a number is the measured onset, `Infinity` an analysed instrumental, `null` un-analysed) read through `silenceTrim.shiftOnsetMs`, because the analyzer measures from byte zero and the drain may be cutting a leading blank off that very track. That composition previously sat inline in `queue.maybePushBed`, whose comment records what a disagreement costs — the prompt told the runway was 2s while the bed decision still thought it was 8s — so `maybePushBed` now reads it from the module too, and a test pins `vocalRunwayMs` equal to `intro-budget.firstVocalMsFor` on the same track. **Three deliberate asymmetries.** A clip at or past the band's ceiling (18s, the intro budget's own upper guard) AIRS rather than deferring, and that test is asked FIRST: no runway inside the binding band could hold a news read, so refusing boundaries for it would only ride the pending slot to the staleness drop and lose it unaired, which is strictly worse than the talk-over it was already doing — long segments are byte-identical to before. A measured onset below the floor (2.5s) refuses every clip, because the floor's leniency belongs to the energy heuristic `intro_ms` and not to a measured vocal. And the IMMEDIATE placement gets no budget at all: it airs mid-track through `say.txt` under the heavy duck (0.22), where there is no runway ahead to land inside — the vocal onset is behind, not in front — and the heavy duck IS the answer already chosen for a placement with no runway. Moving that segment to a vocal-free window is `djTalkOnlyBetweenTracks`, which is the operator's existing switch for exactly this. Un-analysed stations are unaffected end to end: `null` fails open at every gate, so a station with no Demucs pass behaves as it did. Pinned by `scripts/vocal-runway.test.ts`, which goes through a real library DB because `library.get()`'s projection is a hand-written field list and a column dropped from it turns the feature off in silence.
- **Spoken segments are stamped at AIR, not at handoff (#1382)**: `airVoice()` resolves when the clip's path has been written into `say.txt`/`intro.txt`, which is a 0.5s mixer poll plus the request queue, the 0.8s silent lead-in and the duck ramp before a listener hears a word — so every signal keyed off it (booth log, session turn, `dj.say`/`dj.link`) ran ahead of the stream, the player's own line included. The fix is a fourth marker file in the `jingle-playing.json`/`bed-playing.json` family: `airVoice` mints a `voiceId`, stamps it into the clip's `annotate:` URI as `subwave_voice`, and radio.liq's `voice_queue`/`intro_queue` `on_metadata` hooks echo it into `voice-playing.json` as the clip starts feeding. **Every voice clip is annotated now** (a 0 dB trim used to send the bare path) — that is how the id reaches the mixer, and it is the same channel `subsonic_id`/`subwave_kind` already use; the silent lead-in is deliberately left un-annotated, and that missing id is exactly what makes the hook skip it, so the stamp is the first WORD rather than the start of the duck. Two rules hold the timebase together. **The stamp comes from the marker, never from detection** — `startedAt` is the mixer's own clock, so the 500ms reader cadence delays delivery without moving the timestamp; and **it is live-edge like every other timestamp the controller publishes**, so a LISTENER-facing consumer adds `stream.bufferSeconds` (#1114) — which is why `voice.start` carries `streamBufferSeconds` rather than making a relay go and fetch it. Degrading is load-bearing: a controller upgraded ahead of its broadcast image sees no marker file at all, and `awaitVoiceAir` resolves null **immediately** in that case (never waiting out `VOICE_AIR_TIMEOUT_MS`), which is byte-for-byte the old timing — a 20s stall on every booth-log line would be a far worse regression than the early stamp this fixes. An unmeasured air time is reported as `estimated: true` with the timestamps **absent, not zeroed**. Post-air bookkeeping lives in ONE place, `queue.onSpoken()` — the four sites that air a segment (`announce`, `announceExchange`, `airPendingVoice`, `airIntro`) had each inlined their own and had already drifted into three different payloads — and the payload's `channel` is passed by the caller, never re-derived from `kind`, because a boundary-deferred ident is a say-kind clip that deliberately airs on the INTRO channel. The recent-marker buffer ages on **seenAt, not the marker's own stamp** (a mixer clock running behind would otherwise make every marker look instantly expired). `voice.start`/`voice.end` are the new window events; the `dj.*` pair still fires, unchanged in shape and additive in fields, except for banter, where the aggregate one-per-exchange `dj.say` stays and only `voice.*` goes per line. The session turn carries the same stamp as `meta.airedAt`, which is what finally lets the **player** stop showing a line before it is spoken: `useStationFeed` withholds a voice turn until `airedAt + bufferSeconds`, the identical hold it already applies to the track switch, via the pure `splitAudibleTurns`. That hold fails **open** in both directions — a turn with no stamp, or one whose stamp sits implausibly far ahead (a skewed station clock), renders immediately, because "shown early" is the old behaviour while "never shown" would be a new one. Pinned by `scripts/voice-air-events.test.ts`; the mixer half was verified against a real Liquidsoap 2.4.5 (annotate id → `on_metadata`, lead-in skipped, 0 dB path clean). **`voice.queued` is the third event and the only FORECAST in the set**: stamping at air removed the ~1–2s of accidental early warning firing at handoff used to give, which is what a consumer ducking live audio against the station planned with — and `streamBufferSeconds` doesn't give it back to anyone taking station audio ahead of the Icecast burst (a companion app piping the stream into a phone call sits at the live edge, so `voice.start` and the first word arrive together). It fires from `airVoice`'s new synchronous `onQueued` callback, at the moment the rendered clip joins the voice serialiser and BEFORE any of the waiting, carrying the same `voiceId` plus `estimatedAirInMs`/`expectedAirAt`. Three rules keep it honest. The forecast is **named and flagged as one** (`estimated: true`, and deliberately **no `airedAt`** — a field named for a measurement never carries a guess), it is **never corrected afterwards** (`voice.start` is the measurement, and the ids pair), and **nothing in the controller reads it** — no log line, no session turn, no decision, because a forecast that steers behaviour is a second timebase. The wait is computed by the pure `airInEstimate` from the serialiser's own `_chainFreeAt` and `jingleClearAtMs()`, the two deadlines `airVoice` really sleeps out (they OVERLAP — whichever clears last wins — and a stale jingle marker computes a window in the past, so the floor is 0), plus a fixed `HANDOFF_TO_AIR_MS` head for the 0.5s mixer poll and the 0.8s lead-in. So the head start is **however long the wait happens to be** — many seconds behind another segment, ~1s on an idle chain — which is a head start and not a guarantee; don't add a delay to manufacture one. The four air sites now build ONE `SegmentDesc` read by both `onQueued` and `onSpoken`, for the same reason `onSpoken` was centralised in the first place.
- **Beds under a listener request's intro** (`settings.beds.requestIntros`, #1465, default `true` **within** `beds.enabled`, which is itself `false`): a bed decouples the DJ from the song — Song A → bed (DJ talks) → ramp → Song B — and `bed-policy.bedWanted` originally fired it on ONE question, *would this link outlast the incoming track's intro?* That is damage control for a long script, and it is the wrong question for a request: somebody asked for this track, so its opening bars are theirs whether the DJ needs six seconds or twenty. The two live as a **`BedReason`** (`'link' | 'request'`) rather than as a threshold the caller drops to zero, because a request must also outrank the *measured* budget — including an instrumental's `Infinity`, the one value that short-circuits every other `bedWanted` path, which a `thresholdSec: 0` could never beat. `maybePushBed` discriminates on **`requestedBy`, not `introKind`**: all three request paths (`routes/request.ts` ×2, `dj-agent.runRequest`) push `'dj-speak'`, and so does the studio's own bare push, which carries no script and falls out on the next line anyway. **The channel then follows what the clip plays OVER, not its kind** — the same split the boundary-deferred ident already relies on (#1382). A clip airing on a bed has no song to talk over, so it takes `intro.txt`'s LIGHT duck (`ducking.intro`, default `p=0.30`) rather than `say.txt`'s heavy one (`ducking.voice`, default `p=0.22`), which would push the bed — an instrumental put there for exactly this purpose — down to a hiss. That routing is what the pre-#1465 comment meant by "bedding them means reworking the duck routing"; nothing in `radio.liq` changed. **And "airing on a bed" is the CALLER's fact, never `item.bedded`**: the flag only means a bed URI reached `next.txt`, and a pushed item is handed over, never playable — an unresolvable URI is dropped in silence and a marker missed by more than `BED_MARKER_FRESH_MS` never fires the event. In both cases the song itself starts and `onTrackStarted` airs the line over ITS opening, which is a song to talk over and takes the heavy duck like any other request intro. Reading the flag instead would hand the one failure case this feature exists to prevent a *lighter* duck than it had pre-#1465, so `onBedStarted` — the only site that has seen the marker — passes `airIntro({overBed: true})`, exactly as `onSpoken`'s `channel` is passed by whoever knows rather than re-derived. Requests never carry `linkPrev` or `linkClockAt`, so neither `shouldDropStaleLink` nor `linkClockDrifted` can drop the line after the bed is committed and leave it airing naked. **The default is a stated behaviour change, not a byte-identical upgrade**: a fresh station is unaffected (beds off), but a station already running beds gains this at restart, which is the trade the sub-switch exists to make reversible. Pinned by `scripts/bed-policy.test.ts` (the reason's four suppression cases), `scripts/beds-request-intros.test.ts` (a COLD-LOAD round trip — the absent-key case IS the upgrade case) and `scripts/voice-channel.test.ts` (the channel rule, whose load-bearing case is the bed that was queued but never aired).
- **The bed's tail is specified, not left over** (`settings.beds.tailSec`, #1485 FR 5c, default 3s): `bedLengthFor` sizes the bed as `entryCross + head + voice + tail + cross`, and the **cross being a term of that sum is the whole fix**. The bed's `liq_cross_duration` starts the next song fading in at `bedSec - crossSec`, so the quiet an operator actually hears between the DJ's last word and the song is `tail - cross` — and while the sum omitted the cross that quiet was a residual of two settings that know nothing about each other, negative at the shipped defaults (2s tail against a 6s bed ramp put the incoming song under the DJ's closing **four seconds**). A bed exists precisely so the DJ is not talking over the song it is introducing, so the feature inverted itself on a default station, and no value of `crossSec` could fix it without also changing how the song comes in. With the cross in the sum the gap is exactly `tailSec` at every ramp length and every script length, which is what makes it a number an operator can reason about; `scripts/bed-policy.test.ts` asserts that independence across the voice × entry-cross × cross matrix rather than asserting the arithmetic, because the arithmetic is what drifted. **This is a stated behaviour change, not a byte-identical upgrade** — the same trade as #1465's `requestIntros`: a fresh station is unaffected (`beds.enabled` is off by default), a station already running beds gets a longer bed with a clean tail at upgrade, and the old talk-up-to-the-vocal overlap is deliberately **unreachable** (that gesture is the un-bedded path, which `bedWanted` returning false already selects; `tailSec: 0` is the hard end of the range, putting the ramp on the last syllable). Two consequences worth knowing: `bedSec` grew by `cross + tail - 2s`, so a short bed file that used to fit can now miss `pickBed`'s length filter and fall back to talking over the track — the booth log reports the tail alongside `bedSec` for exactly that diagnosis — and `bedLengthFor`'s 1s clamp is now structurally unreachable (`crossSec` is a term of `bedSec`), kept as arithmetic insurance and pinned as inert.
- **The two duck depths are settings, not literals** (`settings.ducking`, #1485 FR 6, defaults `voice: 0.22` / `intro: 0.30`): the numbers are `smooth_add`'s `p` on the two stacked ducking layers, and they ride the ordinary `liquidsoap_*.txt` lifecycle — `writeLiquidsoapSettings` writes `liquidsoap_duck_voice.txt` / `liquidsoap_duck_intro.txt`, `radio.liq` reads each ONCE at mixer startup, so a change flags a restart the way the crossfade does. The defaults are exactly the literals `radio.liq` carried, so an upgrade is byte-identical and a station that never opens the control hears nothing move. **`p` reads backwards from a dB cut**: it is the fraction of the music LEFT UP while that voice channel has signal, so SMALLER is a deeper duck — 1.0 is no duck at all and 0.0 mutes the music under the voice (which is still not the music-PAUSED interlude the same FR names as separate and larger work; the music keeps rolling underneath, silenced). Nothing derives dB from it at a call site, and the admin hint states the direction rather than the conversion, because an operator who reads 0.22 as "22% quieter" tunes it the wrong way. The `[0, 1]` bound is checked in **three** places on purpose and that is not duplication of one rule: the save path REFUSES (the operator typed it, and `ducking.voice`/`ducking.intro` are the dotted `fieldErrors` keys the input highlights itself with), the load path REPAIRS to the shipped depth (a hand-edited `settings.json` must not wedge boot — the split every settings key follows), and `radio.liq` keeps its own range test because the handoff file is the mixer's only input and a hand-edit reaches it with the controller none the wiser, where a value above 1 is a music BOOST under the DJ. The bound itself lives once, as `DUCK_DEPTH_BOUNDS` in `schemas/settings.ts`, which both controller paths read. The `sfx_queue` layer's `p=0.7` stays a literal: a stinger rides beneath both the DJ and the music by design, and its level and depth are one voicing decision rather than a station preference. Pinned by `scripts/duck-depth.test.ts` — including a **cold-load** round trip, since `settings.load()` composes each block explicitly and a field missing from it saves fine, works for the rest of that process, and then reverts on the next restart with nothing in the logs.
- **Listener buffer / live-edge offset (#1114)**: Icecast bursts `settings.stream.bufferSeconds` (default 22s) of already-broadcast audio on connect, so **every listener sits that far behind the live edge for their whole connection**. Two consequences that must stay in sync. (1) `<burst-size>` is a BYTE count, so it's computed **per mount** — `bufferSeconds x that mount's bitrate x 125`, rendered as one `<mount>` block per stream mount by `docker/broadcast-entrypoint.sh` (and the AIO's `render_icecast()`, kept in lockstep), reading `liquidsoap_stream_buffer_seconds.txt` + the per-encoder bitrate files from the state dir; FLAC is VBR so its depth uses a ~900kbps estimate. Never collapse this back to one global figure: the same 528 KB that is 22s of 192k MP3 is ~43s of 96k Opus and ~6s of FLAC (the post-#1114 "Now Spinning runs behind" report). Each mount's `<queue-size>` is 4x its burst (floored at 2 MB) and must stay comfortably above it or clients get evicted the moment they fall behind their own primed buffer. (2) Every timestamp the controller publishes is stamped at the **live edge** (`radio.liq`'s pre-cross `on_metadata`), so listener-facing players shift into listener-time — `useStationFeed` holds the track switch until the audio is actually audible and `trackStartedAt` carries the offset (it can briefly sit in the future; `useElapsed` clamps at 0). The offset the web player applies is the advertised `stream.bufferSeconds` from `/now-playing`, full stop. It used to prefer a per-tab **measurement** (`usePlayer.getListenerLagMs`, `buffered.end − currentTime`) because a byte-sized burst meant a different depth per mount; (1) above fixed that at the source, and the measurement was never the offset anyway. **`buffered.end − currentTime` does NOT measure the listener's distance from the live edge** — it reports only the window the browser has DEMUXED, while Chrome holds the connect burst in an internal cache `buffered` never exposes. Measured directly (tone marker vs `now-playing.json`'s live-edge stamp): true offset **22.5s**, `buffered.end − currentTime` **2.25s**, advertised **22s** — so preferring the measurement flipped every title ~20s early. Don't reintroduce it, and don't use `buffered` to judge cushion health. Operator surfaces (admin dash, MCP) intentionally keep live edge. Changing the depth needs a broadcast **container** bounce, not just a mixer restart — though the telnet `restart` achieves it anyway, since icecast and liquidsoap share a container the entrypoint `wait -n`s on.
- **Listener-count gates (#1256)**: **three** gates fail **OPEN** on an unknown count, and all three read a gated reading, **never the raw `getListenerCount()`** — `listeners.djCallsAllowed()` (all autonomous DJ/LLM work, plus `POST /request`) and the idle-pause monitor (`broadcast/stream-idle.ts`) via `listeners.gatedListenerCount()`, and the analysis quiet gate (`music/analyze-quiet-pure.ts`, whose fail-open runs the OPPOSITE way — an unknown count lets the DSP pass proceed) via `probeListenerCount()`, which returns the gated count from the tagger child's own module state. A failed Icecast status poll is not an observation: the pure `gatedCount` holds the last count actually read from Icecast until failures reach `STALE_STATUS_LIMIT` (4), the same hysteresis the cached `StreamStatus` has carried since #461. On the raw count a **single** `fetchWithTimeout` abort read as "unknown" and the gates opened at once — and because `stream-idle.ts` deliberately forces its own poll every 5s while paused (~120 per 10-minute pause, not the 15s monitor cadence a short manual probe would sample), a sub-1% poll failure rate released nearly every pause ~28s after it engaged: the power save was inactive ~95% of the time it should have been active, and each release also reopened the LLM token gate. Fail-open itself is unchanged — a *sustained* outage still reads null, still resumes, still keeps the DJ on air. Two things keep the failure RATE down rather than absorbing it: the poll is `singleFlight()`-coalesced so the 5s, 15s and on-demand cadences can't race each other into an `AbortError`, and `STATUS_TIMEOUT_MS` is 3000 (not the original 1500) — widening `STALE_STATUS_LIMIT` instead would have slowed #461's offline detection, which is a different contract sharing the same constant. `FIRST_POLL_WAIT_MS` is derived from `STATUS_TIMEOUT_MS`, not written flat, so raising the deadline can't silently un-do the boot await. `presentListeners()` deliberately stays on the RAW count: it fails **CLOSED**, where a blip costs at most one skipped scrobble, which is the safe direction — don't unify it onto the gated reading. `startListenerMonitor()` is awaited before `queue.startWatcher()` so "never polled" can't buy a free agent pick on every restart. Pinned by `scripts/listeners-status.test.ts` and `scripts/stream-idle.test.ts`, which replays 120-poll pause AND live windows through both helpers.
- **Scrobbling, and why Navidrome breaks the pattern (#1298)**: `broadcast/scrobble.ts` fans one `Queue.onTrackStarted` transition out to three independent backends — Last.fm, ListenBrainz and Navidrome — each with its own enable flag, its own credentials and its own failure domain. All three share ONE eligibility rule (Last.fm's: >30s long, and >50% played or >4 minutes; with an unknown duration, `elapsed` IS the effective duration and only the >30s floor applies, because SUB/WAVE has no skip endpoint and a replaced track played to completion), and it lives once in the pure `broadcast/scrobble-pure.ts` — never re-derived per backend. **Navidrome's gate is deliberately different in two ways and neither is an oversight.** It runs BEFORE the `presentListeners()` early-return, because it is the operator's own library rather than a public profile: the whole point of stamping `playCount`/`lastPlayed` is that a `.nsp` smart playlist filtering on `lastPlayed` rotates, and a track the station aired to an empty room still has to stop the picker reaching for it an hour later — a fail-closed listener gate would silently switch the feature off exactly when the station is quietest, which is most of the day. And it addresses a library row by **id**, so an untracked auto-playlist play that reached the mixer without a `subsonic_id` is skipped rather than guessed at from artist+title. Both calls are the same Subsonic `scrobble` endpoint through the shared `music/subsonic.ts` client (salt+token auth, bounded fetch, `/debug` call log): `submission=false` on the incoming track is the now-playing ping and touches no counters, `submission=true` on the outgoing one is the play, stamped with `time` in **milliseconds** since epoch (Subsonic's unit here — Last.fm and ListenBrainz both take seconds) naming when the play STARTED. `settings.scrobble.navidrome.enabled` defaults to **false**, so an upgrade sends nothing; there is nothing else to configure, since the credentials are already `config.navidrome`. Failures log and stop there — no retry, no queue — because the caller is the track-transition path. Pinned by `scripts/scrobble-navidrome.test.ts`.
- **On-air prompt memory follows the editorial session (#1479)**: `Queue.djLog` remains the station-wide booth history, while scripted recap/opener inputs come from persisted post-air `segment` turns. Hard rolls clear current prompt memory and soft autonomous shifts retain it. There are two deliberate handoff directions. An ordinary mic-pass generated after `maybeRoll` gives the sign-off `session.priorPromptMemory()` and the greeting the fresh current session. A final-track boundary handoff is generated before the roll, so the sign-off reads current `session.promptMemory()` and the greeting receives no recap or recent openers. The incoming model therefore never inherits the outgoing topic in either path. Guest attribution continues to ride `exchangeSegment` metadata, and only the final line of a handoff exchange settles its durable lifecycle as aired. Pinned by `scripts/prompt-memory-boundary.test.ts` and `scripts/handoff-memory-wiring.test.ts`.
- **TTS**: callers go through `tts.speak(text, {kind})`, never the engine modules. `tts.js` handles per-kind override + automatic fallback. The Kokoro worker path defaults to `/app/scripts/kokoro_worker.py`; set `KOKORO_WORKER` for non-default layouts. **The rescue chain is a list of voice SLOTS, not engine ids** (`audio/tts-fallback.ts`): the operator's configured fallback (`settings.tts.fallback` — engine AND voice, the TTS analogue of `llm.fallback`, default off), then `defaultEngine` → `piper` → `kokoro`. Only the configured rung carries a `personaTts` override; the hardcoded rungs pass `null`, and that null is load-bearing — a cloud rescue must use the station default's credentials, not the persona provider that just failed. Dedup is by engine, **first-wins**, so a configured fallback naming the same engine as `defaultEngine` keeps its voice rather than reverting to that engine's global default. The same slot serves BOTH triggers (pre-flight reroute in `resolveEngine`, mid-render rescue in `speak`) so there is one answer to "what speaks instead"; `enabled: false` or an absent block is byte-for-byte the pre-fallback behaviour. **A rescue target is engine + provider, not engine (#1345)**: OpenAI, ElevenLabs, Fish and openai-compatible all speak through the one `cloud` dispatcher but are independent failure domains, so `sameTtsTarget` — the ONE place that answers "is this the thing that just failed", never an inline `===` at a call site — compares the provider for `cloud` and the engine alone for everything else. Without it a dead self-hosted Fish server blacklisted a healthy configured ElevenLabs rescue and the station dropped to Piper. An unspecified provider on either side resolves to `settings.tts.cloud.provider`, which is what keeps a station that names no provider anywhere byte-identical. Note the asymmetry with dedup, which stays ENGINE-keyed: a failed provider must not blacklist a healthy sibling, but only ONE cloud rung is ever attempted per rescue — a second is another network round-trip on the same dispatcher while the local floor waits below. Both triggers and both `fellBack` reports (`recordTts`, `describeRouting`) run the same comparison, because an engine-only one reports a fish→ElevenLabs reroute as no fallback at all and hides the misconfiguration Stats and `/debug` exist to show. Pinned by `scripts/tts-fallback.test.ts`.
- **A persona's engine may be the `inherit` sentinel — resolve it at ONE seam.** `PERSONA_TTS_INHERIT` ("use `settings.tts.defaultEngine`") exists because `defaultEngine` was otherwise dead for any station with personas: every seeded persona pinned `piper`, a pinned engine beats the station default, and the slot schema refuses an empty engine, so an operator who switched the station to the cloud voice still heard Piper with nothing in the logs. It is deliberately **not** in `TTS_ENGINES` — that list is also the vocabulary for `defaultEngine` itself (which would inherit from itself), for the per-engine `gainDb`/`speed` maps, and for the rescue slot (where it would name the rung *below* it) — and `ttsVoiceSlotSchema`/`repairTtsVoiceSlot` admit it only behind `allowInherit`, which only the persona call sites pass. `djPersonaTts()` resolves it via `resolvePersonaVoiceSlot()` before anything downstream compares an engine id; the two readers that bypass that seam (`cloud-speech.ts`'s three `*ForPersona` entry points, `djSystem()`'s chatterbox hint) resolve for themselves, because each keys off one specific engine and a raw sentinel answers "no" to all of them — a miss that reads as "pinned elsewhere" and is therefore silent. **The voice half is the one with teeth**: a voice id on an inherit slot was chosen without knowing the engine, so it carries only to `TTS_INHERITABLE_VOICE_ENGINES` (piper and kokoro — the pair the schema already treats as one id-space, #454). chatterbox reads the field as a reference `.wav` filename, pocket-tts as a built-in id, cloud/remote as provider-specific names; handing any of them a Kokoro-shaped id is a missing file, a 400, or a silent substitution, so they take the station's voice or their own default. The lenient load path never repairs *to* the sentinel and never invents a voice *for* it — both would re-point a persona written before it existed. Pinned by `scripts/persona-engine.test.ts` (the pure rule) and `scripts/persona-engine-seams.test.ts` (the call sites that must resolve).
- **openai-compatible TTS extra params** (`settings.tts.cloud.compatParams`, #1317, default `[]` = off): free-form `{key, value}` body fields merged into the POST to `/audio/speech`, for knobs a self-hosted server defines and the OpenAI API doesn't (Chatterbox's `temperature`/`seed`/`exaggeration`, and whatever the next engine invents — a named setting per server generation doesn't scale). **The merge happens at the fetch layer** (`compatBodyFetch` in `cloud-speech.ts`), not via `providerOptions`: the AI SDK's OpenAI speech model builds a CLOSED body, and its `providerOptions.openai` hatch both validates against a 2-key schema AND (in `@ai-sdk/openai` 4.0.11) drops what it parses through an upstream loop over an empty object — rewriting the serialized body is the only hook that works short of forking the provider. Rules live ONLY in `settings/compat-params.ts`, shared by the validator and the send path so a key that saves is a key that sends; values are stored as text and coerced to JSON types at send time (`0.8` → number, `007` → string, because servers use such values as ids). Four keys are **reserved**: `model`/`voice`/`input` have first-class fields (and an `input` override would mute the segment while still "succeeding"), and `speed` would COMPOUND with the local ffmpeg atempo stretch compat uses instead of server-side speed (#942). The list must stay in `settings.load()`'s explicit `tts.cloud` composition — that block does NOT spread `DEFAULTS`, so a key missing there saves to disk and then silently vanishes on the next restart. Empty keeps the request byte-identical. Pinned by `scripts/compat-tts-params.test.ts`.
- **Daily token budget** (`settings.llm.dailyTokenCap`, default `0` = off): the running per-UTC-day token tally lives in `llm/internal/telemetry/budget.ts` (incremented from the single `record()` chokepoint in `telemetry/log.ts`, the same `ok && usage.total` guard as the lifetime ticker; seeded once at boot from today's `events-*.jsonl` so a restart resumes the count). The pure `budgetMode()` (`core/pure.ts`, unit-pinned) maps `{used, cap, softPct}` → `normal | soft | hard`; **policy lives only in `broadcast/dj-budget.ts`** (the call-site gates), never inline. Tiers: `soft` (≥`budgetSoftPct`%) forces the cheap pool picker and mutes optional segments (links, station IDs, hourly, weather/news/etc.); `hard` (≥cap) makes **no** model call — `runTrackEvent` returns early so Liquidsoap coasts on the LLM-free `auto.m3u` (music never stops). Listener requests stay exempt through the hard cap unless `llm.exemptRequests` is off. Manual `/dj/segment` command routes (the gate-free `runStationId`/`runHourlyCheck`/`runLink` runners) are intentionally **not** budget-gated — an explicit operator action always fires.

Running the tool loop unconditionally was the bug: pool mode exists for models not trusted with tool loops, so a grounded co-hosted skill could never clear its grounding check there (the tool is reachable only by a model tool call) and stood down every tick after burning a full agent run against `agentTimeoutMs`. Neither thing that SETS the gap lands on the clock: an ident is scheduled at :15/:45 but boundary-DEFERRED to the next transition (a :15 ident airing at :19:35 is ordinary), and the segment director ticks every 5 minutes with a **zero** floor on `aggressive` (`skills/_agent.ts` `frequencyFloorMs`). Either one lands in the fixed tick's 5-minute shadow, the tick stood down, and the next chance was 30 minutes away — on `moderate`, which owns only the :20 slot, the whole hour. Observed as hours of eligible guest shows airing no banter, so the more talkative the station, the LESS banter it could produce. The slot still opens at :20/:50 (minutes no other wall-clock talker owns — issue #310) but the tick now runs every minute for `BANTER_WINDOW_MINUTES` (10, twice the gap, and short of the :30/:00 ident slots) and fires the first minute the gap is genuinely clear, so a :19:35 ident POSTPONES the exchange to :24:35 instead of cancelling it. Three rules hold it: the gap itself is **untouched** (classifying short idents as not-real-talk is the tempting fix and the wrong one — it lets banter stack right behind one, which is what the gap is for); **one fire per slot**, keyed by `talkSlotKey`, and the claim is taken BEFORE the await, since rendering a multi-voice exchange outlasts a minute and the next tick would otherwise start a concurrent one; and the window is a ROW in the talk-slot table (`broadcast/talk-scheduler.ts`, #1500) rather than a cron expression of its own, so the schedule cannot drift from the window constants — the one per-minute `talkTick` covers every minute any row can want. The frequency ladder stays in `dj-gate.ts` with the other rungs and asks the TABLE only which slot a minute belongs to — that indirection is what makes :24 read as the :20 slot, so `moderate` keeps its retry without gaining a second exchange, and it is the same question the ident rung now asks (`openMinuteFor(talkSlot('station-id'), m)`) for the same reason. **The stand-down is now LOGGED** with the reason and the numbers (`[banter] stood down at :20 — last standalone talk 25s ago, minimum gap 300s (retrying until :29)`, plus one line when a window closes unfired), once per slot rather than once per tick: the silent `return` is why an hour of starved banter left nothing in the booth log to explain itself. The tick's own state machine is the pure **`talkTickPlan`** (`banterTickPlan` until #1500 generalised it to every talk row) — the caller resolves ONE `eligible` flag per row from the live modules (roster, frequency rung, listeners, budget) and the planner owns the window, the gap and the logging, the same split as `skillCronAllowed` and for the same reason: a rule that reads live settings state itself cannot be pinned. What stayed in `banter-policy.ts` is the NUMBERS and their reasoning, plus the window constants `dj-gate`'s rung reads through `openMinuteFor(talkSlot('banter'), m)`. Pinned by `scripts/talk-scheduler.test.ts`, which replays the reporter's own hour minute by minute (a :19:35 ident → the exchange airs at :25, one stand-down line, not ten) and was mutation-checked against a 1-minute window, a removed log-once guard and a removed slot claim; `scripts/banter-policy.test.ts` keeps the window constants and the rung.
- **The talk tick** (`broadcast/talk-scheduler.ts` + `scheduler.talkTick`, #1500): every SCHEDULED spoken segment is a ROW in one slot table driven by one `* * * * *` cron — the hourly check (:00), programme beats (station clock, sampled on a 5-minute stride), banter (:20/:50 windows) and idents (:15/:30/:45). They were four independent crons, coordinating only implicitly through hand-partitioned minutes, and that partitioning has already produced two bugs: #310 (ident + hourly both at :00, two voice segments stacked back to back — fixed by moving idents off :00) and #1419 (banter's slot evaluated at an instant — fixed by turning it into a window). Only banter got the second fix, so the table is where the rest can get it. Each row carries `opens`/`windowMinutes`/`minGapMs`/`air`/`priority`/`clock`/`stride`, and four of those are load-bearing distinctions a merge must not unify away: `air: 'next-track'` is the ident's boundary deferral (with its daypart stamp, so a stale clock fact is refused) against everyone else's immediate air; `clock: 'station'` is the programme beat's zone-relative placement against process-minute slots (station zones sit at :30/:45 offsets, so a fixed process minute lands mid-show); `stride` keeps the programme row sampled where its `*/5` cron sampled it; and `minGapMs: 0` means **no gap check at all**, not a check that usually passes — an ident 25s behind a segment fires today, and generalising banter's gap onto every row would be a behaviour change, not a refactor. Three things the tick deliberately does NOT own. **Eligibility**: the frequency ladder (`dj-gate`), listeners, the daily budget, the roster and `programme.onAir()` are all still resolved at fire time through their own modules, lazily and only for a row whose window is open — the table carries placement, not policy. **The :00 session roll**: `rollSessionNow({airHandoff:false})` shared the hourly cron and ran BEFORE its gates, so it is not a talk action and runs unconditionally ahead of the planner — gating it behind the hourly row would stop a muted, empty or over-budget station rolling its session, planning its episode and leaving the handoff pending. **How often the segment director speaks**: it IS a row — the table's one `fill` row — but only the offer is the table's; the per-kind cooldowns and the frequency floor stay in `skills/_agent.ts`. Manual `/dj/segment` runners (`runHourlyCheck`, `runBanter`, `runStationId`, `runProgramme*`) stay entirely outside the scheduler and gate-free — an operator press always fires. **The table shipped inert and was then turned on**, in two PRs. The first carried windows of one minute and zero gaps — byte-identical to the four crons — so the merge could be reviewed as a merge. The second gave every row a real window (ten minutes for idents, ten for the hourly check, banter's unchanged) and a real quiet gap (three minutes for the short segments, banter's five for the long one), which is what generalises #1419's postpone-don't-cancel from banter to everything: an ident that loses :15 to a segment retries through :24 instead of waiting for :30, and on `quiet` — one ident an hour, at :45 — instead of waiting for the next hour. Three rules hold the widened table together. **One talker per minute**: windows now OVERLAP by design (the ident's :15 window reaches into banter's :20), so #310 stops being a property of four cron strings and becomes a rule in `talkTickPlan` — the highest-priority firing row takes the minute and every other one waits inside its own window. Priority is ordered by a principle, not a preference: a row that CANNOT retry outranks one that can (the programme beat leads — `dueBeat` is sampled once, so a held beat is a lost beat, and its log line says exactly that rather than promising a retry), and among rows that can, fewer remaining chances outranks more (hourly, then banter, then idents). **A row yields only to a row that is FIRING**, never to one that is merely open — yielding to an open-but-blocked row would let a high-priority row sit on its whole window holding everything beneath it, which is #1419 again one layer up, and is the single subtlest line in the module. **In-flight talk counts, but never past the row's last chance**: `queue.pendingVoiceTalk()` reports the kind and enqueue time of the segment already rendered and queued for the next boundary (`_pendingVoice`), which is talk that has NOT aired and which `getLastTalkBreakAt()` therefore cannot see — the blind spot that let a :20 tick read a clear gap and still land ten seconds in front of a :15 ident. A gap-gated row stands down for that clip, and the hold is **bounded at both ends** (#1539), because `_pendingVoice` may legitimately live `PENDING_VOICE_MAX_AGE_MS` (20 minutes) and is dropped only at a track start — so on a station where every boundary carries its own link, an unbounded hold outlasts the window it is holding and quietly turns a postpone into a cancel. The first bound is the clip's own life: one that will expire *before or exactly when* the window closes is dropped by the queue while the row still has minutes left, so it never blocks at all and the row falls straight through to the normal aired-talk gap (equality deliberately favours the scheduled row). The second is the row's remaining chances: a clip that *outlives* the window blocks only while `canRetry` is true, so the window's **last minute is the row's final chance and is never given away**. Both are needed, and the reason is that the first comparison cancels `now` out — `queuedAt + MAX > close` is constant across the whole window, so on its own it holds a row for every minute or none, which is what let an ident retrying at :51 (inside its own :45 window, which runs to :54) hold the hourly check — a row with **one chance an hour** — through all of :00–:09. Taking that last minute may land in front of a clip that airs seconds later; a stacked break the operator can hear beats an hourly check that silently never happened. The two numbers live once each: `pendingVoiceStale()` and `pendingVoiceValidForMs()` sit beside the constant in `broadcast/queue/kinds.ts`, read by the queue's drop and the scheduler's hold respectively, so neither re-spells the arithmetic. Queue still owns eventual stale dropping at a boundary, and this changes neither the one-talker-per-minute rule nor rows with `minGapMs: 0`, which have opted out of quiet questions entirely. It also closes a quieter bug while the pending clip remains plausible: the :30 ident cron used to fire while the :15 ident was still pending, and `announceAtNextTrack` keeps ONE slot, so the older ident was silently replaced. Every hold is now **logged once per slot with its cause and its numbers** — `[station-id] stood down at :15 — last standalone talk 60s ago, minimum gap 180s (retrying until :24)`, `… — a station-id is rendered and waiting for the next track boundary …`, `… — banter took the minute …`, plus one `slot :15 missed` line when a window closes unfired. Before this, only banter said anything; an ident or a time check that collided with off-clock talk simply did not happen, and the booth log recorded an absence. **The segment director joined as a FILL row** rather than a fifth slot. It is the one talker with no wall-clock placement — a `*/5` cron with a frequency floor of ZERO on `aggressive` — and it ticked on :00, :15, :20, :30, :45 and :50, every opening minute the other rows own. `banter-policy.ts` names it as one of the two things that could air off-clock and starve a slot, so half of #1419 is its doing; the window fixed the other half. A fill row differs from a slot row in exactly two ways, and both follow from having no scheduled chance to lose. It **stands down whenever a slot row wants the minute** — including one merely *waiting*, because a filler that speaks resets the quiet gap and pushes that row's retry further out, possibly past its window; a slot row deliberately does NOT yield that widely, since a row that yields to a merely-open row can be starved by it. And it **stands down silently**: a filler that did not fill is not an event, where a scheduled segment quietly not happening is #1419 itself. "Wants the minute" is deliberately narrower than "has an open window", and the arithmetic is the reason: with ten-minute windows the slot rows cover 50 of the 60 minutes, so deferring to open WINDOWS would leave the director `:10` and `:40` — two ticks an hour instead of six. That is not standing down, it is switching off, and it is what the issue's original wording ("fires only when no fixed slot is open or pending") would have produced once the windows widened. A row that has already fired its slot, or is ineligible this minute, produces no plan and wants nothing. The offer is also resolved in two passes rather than one filtered pass, so on a contested minute the director's own four gates are never consulted — the same gate-before-generation reasoning that keeps `speak()` out of the gating path. The cost is real and is pinned as a number: in a clean chatty hour the director keeps `:05 :10 :25 :35 :40 :55` and loses the six opening minutes. Those six are precisely the minutes it used to double-talk on, so the halving IS the fix — and its own floor already limits how often it takes an offer, so on `moderate` and below the lost ticks cost almost no airtime. The headline property, and the first thing `scripts/talk-scheduler.test.ts` pins: **a quiet hour is unchanged** — with room in the hour every row still fires on the minute its old cron did, for every frequency rung. The windows and gaps cost nothing when nothing is in the way; they only act when something is. The test also walks a disrupted hour (a segment at :14 pushes the ident to :17 and the exchange to :22), the reporter's own #1419 hour, both arbitration directions, the yield-only-to-a-firing-row rule and the pending-voice hold.
- **The automatic jingle rotate is a talk-table row** (`settings.jingleRotate`, #1619, default `'mixer'` = the pre-existing station; `broadcast/jingle-rotate.ts`): Liquidsoap decided on its own when a rotate jingle played — `rotate(weights=[1, jingle_ratio()])`, one stinger every N tracks — and the controller only learned about it afterwards, through `jingle-playing.json`. The collision guard built on that marker (#997, #1258, #1468) is a HOLD, so spoken segments wait for the jingle's window to pass; that stops the station ID talking over the DJ but leaves the planner unable to plan around a jingle it never scheduled, which is how a link written for the next boundary lands right behind a stinger and a scheduled ident sits behind a jingle that took the same seam. With `'controller'` the count moves into the controller, which already sees every boundary through `now-playing.json`, and the stinger is handed over through the existing single writer (`queue.playRotateJingle()` draws the clip, `queue.playJingle()` writes `jingle-now.txt`); `settings/liquidsoap.ts` writes the mixer's ratio 0, already #997's documented "jingles off" value, so **`radio.liq` is untouched by this feature** and both image-skew directions stay correct — a new controller switches an old mixer's rotate off through a file that mixer already honours, and an old controller drives a new broadcast image exactly as today. **The upgrade shape is the reason it is opt-in.** The issue offered the alternative of shipping it as the only mode with the ratio pinned to 0 on the same release, and that cannot satisfy its own rule (an upgraded station must not lose its jingles or get them twice) because the two containers upgrade independently and the ratio file is read ONCE at mixer startup: pinning the file for everyone races the mixer's boot read on a `up -d --build`, and pinning it inside `radio.liq` silences every station whose controller is still the old image. Opt-in has neither moment; the residual cost — a station that flips the toggle and does not restart the mixer runs both rotates until it does, and **may hear jingles more often than configured** — is the existing contract for this exact file, which is what the control's "needs restart" wording has always been for, and it is paid on an operator action rather than on an upgrade. The MAGNITUDE of that skew is deliberately stated as "more often" rather than "twice", and is still unmeasured over a long run: an on-air check of the un-restarted state saw 2 jingles against 2 controller fires over three tracks — 1:1, not 2:1 — which `radio.liq` explains, since the mixer's own rotate carries `not jingle_now_on_air()` and `time() > voice_until()` and therefore stands down while a controller-handed stinger is feeding. Three tracks is far too thin to call it a property either way, and nothing about the design conclusion turns on the number: the argument against the only-mode shape is that its skew is INVOLUNTARY and arrives on an upgrade, not that it is exactly double. **It is a `fill` row, not a slot**, and this is the same argument the segment director's role is rather than a weaker version of it: a fill row is one with no scheduled chance to lose, and a rotate's due-ness is a COUNT (`queue.rotateJingleTracksSince()`) that keeps counting while the row waits, so a minute given away costs a minute and nothing else. A slot row here would be wrong twice — it would suppress the director on every minute the jingle merely waited, and every hold would log `missed` about a chance that was never lost, since an `opens: 'any'` row's window is one minute wide and `canRetry` is therefore false on all of them. What the issue asked for ("the segment director stands down for it the way it does for an ident") is what one talker per minute already does between two fill rows: the higher-priority plan takes the minute and the other waits. Priority is LAST, by the table's own principle — among rows that can retry, fewer remaining chances outranks more, and the director is offered twelve minutes an hour against this row's sixty. Three more distinctions are deliberate. The row carries the short segments' three-minute quiet gap, which is the half of #310 it can answer (a stinger must not land on the back of a break that just finished); the other half, talk landing on the back of the STINGER, is not the planner's and stays in the `jingle-playing.json` hold and `radio.liq`'s `voice_until` gate on the priority queue — which is why that guard survives untouched, and also why the gap is one-directional (a jingle is not talk, so it never appears in `getLastTalkBreakAt()`). Its `air` is `'immediate'` because that describes the HANDOFF: the write happens now and Liquidsoap's track-sensitive `jingle_now_queue` places the clip at the next safe boundary, so claiming `'next-track'` would say the controller is deferring something it is not. And the pending-clip hold is UNBOUNDED for it, where #1539 bounds it for a row with a finite window — the bound exists only to stop a hold turning a postpone into a cancel, there is nothing here to cancel, and the bounded form would read as "never held at all" on a row whose every minute is its own window. That generalisation is guarded on `minGapMs > 0`, so the director (which has opted out of quiet questions entirely) is unmoved. Two more: eligibility carries **none** of the DJ gates — a stinger costs no tokens and no TTS, and neither the mixer's rotate nor an operator press has ever been listener-gated or muted by `tts.enabled` (`voice-policy.ts` names `jingleRatio: 0` as the way to silence jingles precisely because the voice switch does not) — and `runTalkSlot` skips its heavy-TTS warm for this row, since the clip is already on disk and warming for it would reload a model nothing is about to use. A rotate that comes due and cannot be drawn (empty library, the same clip still pending) SPENDS the offer rather than banking it: that is what `radio.liq`'s `source.available` gate did ("skipping a jingle is the cheaper miss"), and banking it would leave the row due every subsequent minute, holding the seam against the director until the library was filled or a press aged out. Advance notice to the DJ script ("after the station ID") is deliberately out of scope. Pinned by `scripts/jingle-rotate.test.ts`, and the table's own properties by `scripts/talk-scheduler.test.ts`.
- **Talk only between tracks** (`djTalkOnlyBetweenTracks`, default **false**, #1485 FR 5b): one switch that forces EVERY scheduled spoken segment onto the boundary the station ident has always waited for, so nothing ducks a song mid-play. Off is the pre-existing station byte for byte — an absent or non-boolean key coerces false in `load()`, and the table keeps its per-row `air` modes. On, the planner resolves every row to `'next-track'`, the fill row and the hourly check included; **the hourly check is the deliberate non-exemption** (a station that never talks over a song cannot make an exception for the one segment that reads a clock), and the trade is stated on the toggle rather than engineered away: a held check can air a track later than the minute it was written for, bounded by the daypart stamp (`stationIdDaypartDrifted` refuses a clip whose part of the day has moved on) and by `PENDING_VOICE_MAX_AGE_MS`, not by the 90s `linkClockDrifted` tolerance, which belongs to links. Two seams carry it, and each is a seam rather than a parameter for a stated reason. **Placement is decided in the planner** — `betweenTracksOnly` in, `TalkPlan.air` out — because the dispatcher acts on the plan and a second resolver anywhere else is the duplicate-policy bug; `broadcast/talk-air.ts` owns only the READ of the setting. **Delivery is an `AsyncLocalStorage` SCOPE** (`withTalkAir`/`currentTalkAir`, same module) that `scheduler.runTalkSlot` wraps every fire in, and which `queue.announce()` / `queue.announceExchange()` act on. The alternative — a flag threaded from the tick to each runner — would make the constraint a property of eight call sites (the segment director alone speaks from four inside `skills/_agent.ts`, programme beats from two more in `programme.ts`) that a ninth could silently miss; the scope makes it a property of the DISPATCH, so a skill written after this shipped defers without knowing the setting exists. It is also what keeps **manual triggers exempt with no second rule**: `/dj/segment` and `/dj/skill` call the same gate-free runners from OUTSIDE any scope, which is the same mechanism (never reaching the gate) that already exempts them from the voice switch, the clock switch and the frequency ladder. `_pendingVoice` stays **one slot**, and gained a `clips` LIST rather than a queue of segments: a banter exchange is several lines in several voices and defers as ONE segment, aired back to back at the boundary with each line keeping its speaker's attribution (`exchangeSegment`) and the exchange keeping its single `dj.say` webhook, now fired at air time. Every deferred segment rides the INTRO file (light duck) whatever its kind, which is the deferred path's existing rule and not `voiceChannelFor`'s. **A second scheduled segment is POSTPONED, never queued**: the planner holds every row while a live clip is waiting — including rows with `minGapMs: 0`, which have opted out of quiet questions but not out of physics — because firing would REPLACE the waiting clip (one slot, newer wins) and delete a segment already paid for in tokens and TTS. Postponing at the planner is also gate-before-generation: the held row writes no script at all. **The one rule that inverts is #1539's last-minute release.** With the switch off, the window's final minute is never given away, trading a stacked break for a kept slot; with it on that trade does not exist (the row would defer too), so the hold stands through the last minute and the row logs `missed` — honest, and rare, since the boundary it is waiting for is usually a track away. The sharp edge is the programme beat: `dueBeat` is sampled once, so a beat held by a waiting clip is lost, and its log line says `and it has no second chance` rather than promising a retry. Pinned by `scripts/talk-scheduler.test.ts` (the switch both ways, the no-gap rows, the release, the inverted last minute, one-talker-per-minute intact) and `scripts/talk-air.test.ts` (the cold-load coercion and the scope's manual-trigger exemption). **Two spoken moments are deliberately outside the switch**, because they are not talk-table rows: the persona MIC-PASS, which the :00 roll leaves pending for the next track boundary anyway (`airHandoff: false`), and the programme INTRO, which rides the session-settled hook — normally fired by `queue.onTrackStarted`'s look-ahead, i.e. already at a boundary, and left immediate on the :00 fallback path because the roll must stay ungated and a deferred intro would collide with `airPendingVoice`'s handoff-supersedes rule (the intro would be dropped, not moved). The switch is on the admin TTS section's Station voice card, beside the voice switch, and on `/debug` as `llm.talkAir`.
- **Pause-and-talk breaks** (`shows[].pauseTalk`, opt-in per show; `settings.pauseTalkMinSeconds`, station-wide, default 20s; #551): ducking is right for a short aside, but a 30-second skill segment deserves a real gap. When an eligible segment's RENDERED clip clears the threshold, `queue.announce()` holds it and the drain writes a **silent item** onto the music timeline ahead of the next track; the mixer's `pause-talk-playing.json` marker then releases the clip down the ordinary `say.txt` path. **The voice never rides the music path**, and that is the whole design: `radio.liq` applies `mic_chain` and `edge_fade` to `voice_queue`/`intro_queue` only, so a clip sent through `next.txt` would be the one segment on the station with no compression, no makeup gain and no edge fade — audibly unlike everything around it — and would need a parallel set of air-time stamps. The silence is what buys the gap; the voice keeps its chain, its `voice-playing.json` stamp and its `onSpoken` bookkeeping unchanged. Five things are load-bearing. **The hold reuses `_pendingVoice`**, the one slot the talk planner already reads through `queue.pendingVoiceTalk()` — a second place to park unaired speech is exactly how a held segment and a station ident both get cleared for the same boundary, which is what the talk table exists to prevent (#1505). The slot remains occupied through the crossfade release delay and actual voice start, so another scheduled line cannot steal the reserved gap. **The silence is budgeted, not guessed** (`broadcast/pause-talk.ts` `silenceDurationMs`): `cross` sizes a transition from the OUTGOING track's `liq_cross_duration` (`dj_transition` reads `a.metadata`), so the previous song is still fading over the HEAD of the silence for its own crossfade — 10s on the station default. Un-budgeted, the whole segment lands inside that fade and plays under decaying music, which is the ducking the feature exists to replace. So the silence covers incoming cross + release latency + voice window + exit ramp + slack, and `releaseDelayMs` waits the same crossfade out before opening the mic, measured from the silence's own `startedAt` so the marker poll already spent is credited rather than paid twice. The budget uses the station's `crossfadeDuration` rather than the outgoing track's own stamp — that stamp is on a track already handed to the mixer, and every stamp the controller writes is capped at the setting (`music/mix.ts`), so it is the safe upper bound; over-estimating costs a little extra silence, which is the recoverable direction. `pauseTimelineDelayMs` subtracts the overlapping entry/exit crosses and stamps the net delay on the following queue item; listener ETAs and show-boundary cuts then count that hidden item exactly as they count a bed. **An ARMED break is committed, not a forecast**: before `next.txt` is written, the matching voice, id, timing and optional SFX are atomically stored in `state/pause-talk/pending.json`, independent of queue.json's debounce. `queue.recover()` restores that commitment even when there is no queue snapshot; once the silence is handed over, `airPendingVoice` and `dropPendingVoice` both stand off it and `onPauseTalkStarted` owns the release. Airing it early is not merely a double-delivery — a pair drain sends two items, so the silence can be armed ahead of item N while N-1 is still sent-but-unaired, and airing the clip ducked over N-1 leaves the silence to play as a 20–90s hole. That commitment is bounded (`PAUSE_TALK_ARM_MAX_AGE_MS`): a silence that never reaches the mixer disarms and the clip falls back to ordinary ducked delivery, because a pause-and-talk failure before voice publication must cost the break, never the segment — which is also what a listener request or a stem blend owning the seam does. **Speech release is an idempotent four-phase handoff**, not “delete the commit after writing `say.txt`”: the controller publishes a stable 16-hex delivery id on the voice URI; `poll_voice` writes `pause-talk-voice-accepted.json` only after `voice_queue.push` returns; `voice_marker` writes `pause-talk-voice-started.json` at the actual live edge; and only after `onSpoken` does the controller write `pauseAcknowledgedAt` into the commitment before deleting it. A restarted controller uses the strongest phase it can prove (`started` → generic stable-id `voice-playing.json` → `accepted` → still present in `say.txt`) and **never republishes a phase the mixer owns**. If it sees none, it waits out the mixer's 0.5s poll/read/push/atomic-marker interval before publishing. With the shared state volume writable, a controller-only crash with the mixer alive therefore gives exactly-once audio before publication, after publication, after mixer consumption, at voice start and through controller cleanup. It deliberately does not promise a distributed transaction across simultaneous process loss: if the mixer dies after accepting but before starting, its in-memory queue is gone while its durable acceptance remains, and replaying would trade lost speech for possible duplicates. A first-version pause mixer has the stable generic start marker but no acceptance marker, so the same recovery is exact once publication is still visible or start is visible; its consume-before-start interval is only the bounded best effort. **`pause_talk_on_air` gates the jingle rotate** on both halves of the break, exactly as `bed_on_air` does, so no stinger splits the gap from the song it leads into. The selected SFX travels with the pending voice and is handed over only when that voice is; curiosity's durable aired ledger likewise waits for post-air completion. Silence items are ~3 MB apiece and nothing plays them twice: `cleanupPauseTalkSilence` sweeps them hourly beside `cleanupOldVoices`, age-based rather than unlinked at air time because the mixer still holds the file open while it plays it. Only the two single-speaker skill-segment `announce()` call sites pass `pauseTalkEligible`; programme features and multi-speaker exchanges explicitly opt out, so links, banter, programme beats, hourly checks, idents and request intros retain their prior placement.

- **The auto-playlist follows the SHOW, not the hour (#1111)**: `auto.m3u` is steered by the resolved active show — genres, era windows, energies, moods, vocals, `filtersStrict`, the pinned and excluded Navidrome playlists, the length cap — but `refreshAutoPlaylist` only ever ran at boot and on the `autoQueueRefreshMinutes` cron (default 60). A show change landing between two ticks left the previous show's file on disk, so with the live queue empty the station coasted on it: the report's Playlist Only (Strict) show had **26 of 28** fallback entries outside its pinned playlist until the operator pressed Refresh, after which all 16 were inside it. Strict is enforced correctly whenever the file is rebuilt; nothing was rebuilding it. The fix hooks the SHARED BOUNDARY SEQUENCE (`scheduler.rollSessionNow`) rather than adding a second cron — every one of the four transitions the report names already runs through it (a scheduled show starting or ending via the :00 talk tick, a takeover start and early cancel via `routes/shows.ts`, an expiry via `overrideJanitor`), so a fifth path cannot be added without inheriting the rebuild, and the rebuild is not on a timetable a takeover starting on the operator's minute can miss. Each call site names its own `reason`, which is what the booth log prints. Three rules in `broadcast/auto-playlist-show.ts` (pinned by `scripts/auto-playlist-show.test.ts`) and none of them is optional: **coming OFF a show is a change** — an id-only key leaves the ended show's strict fallback playing over default programming, which is the second half of every scheduled boundary; **the key covers every field the pool build reads, not the id**, so editing the live show's pinned playlist rebuilds too, while sorted lists mean a genre re-order (which cannot change the pool) does not spend a Navidrome fan-out; and **the stamp records a build that LANDED** — a refresh that threw left the previous show's entries on disk, so stamping anyway would let the next boundary read the stale file as current and restore the original bug. The claim taken before the await is only for concurrency (an expiry sweep against an operator's cancel), and the landed build overwrites it with whatever show the refresh actually resolved. The hook is fire-and-forget with its own trap: it is Navidrome I/O, and holding the mic-pass behind a pool rebuild would duck the outro the handoff is supposed to land on.

- **Programmes** (`shows[].programme`, opt-in; optional `shows[].segmentSkill` pins the feature): the show airs as a produced episode — intro at the top, one feature beat per hour (station-minute :35–:39 window), outro in the final hour's closing minutes (station-minute :55–:59 by default, moved by `handover.offsetMinutes` — see the handover bullet below) (`broadcast/programme.ts`; a single `*/5` cron dispatches on `dueBeat()`'s STATION-zone minute — fixed process-minute crons land mid-show on :30/:45-offset zones like IST; manual `runProgramme*` runners in `scheduler.ts`). Coherence comes from a per-episode **producer plan** (one `generateProgrammePlan` `djObject` call at session start: angle, per-hour feature topics, intro/outro notes) stored ON the session (`session.attachProgramme`); beats are marked aired BEFORE generating (the `markHandoffAired` idempotency pattern) so restarts never double-air. Plan-call failure → `fallback`: beats still air from the raw topic brief. Collision rules: the intro replaces the hourly check in hour one (and on a persona-change boundary the handoff **greeting** doubles as the intro — `episodeAngle` rides into `generateHandoffGreeting`; the standalone intro is skipped); `skillsTick` stands down for the whole programme (`programme.onAir()`); idents/banter keep their slots. The feature runs through the forced segment director (`runCapability(kind, ctx, {brief, persona})`) so it gets real data; a stale/absent kind falls to straight talk (`generateProgrammeFeature`), and so does a capability that **stands down** because its data came back unusable (`aired: false`, `skills/abstain-policy.ts`, #1412) — the beat is mandatory, the invented facts are not. Guest shows open/close with multi-voice exchanges (`generateProgrammeExchange` → `announceExchange`). **Every beat carries `PROGRAMME_GROUNDING_RULE`** (`llm/internal/prompts/programme.ts`, #1301) — one constant appended by all four, never inlined per beat. `PROGRAMME_CONTEXT_FIELDS` shows these prompts NO track fields, so a beat told to be "concrete" about a music topic can only reach for a record it knows from training: a straight-talk feature aired "Smells Like Teen Spirit … blasts in" 16s into an unrelated track. The fix bans the **cue framing** ("playing now / coming up / just heard"), NOT the noun — a grunge feature that can't name *Nevermind* isn't a feature — and asks for certainty as a **separate** half, because real-facts-framed-as-a-cue and invented-credits are independent failure modes. The plan's `featureKindsClause` scopes `kind: null` to topics the host can carry unaided, but that is a frequency lever only: `runFeature` still reaches the ungrounded generator whenever a capability throws at air time. Pure arc helpers (`showSpan`, `planFeature`) live in `broadcast/programme-pure.ts`, pinned by `scripts/programme.test.ts`; the grounding rule + per-beat builders by `scripts/programme-grounding.test.ts`.
- **Show-boundary handoffs** (`broadcast/session.ts`, `broadcast/queue.ts`): look-ahead may select the first incoming-show track, but it cannot roll the live session or speak the handoff. The boundary record captures the final outgoing track, incoming identity, target context, and any prepared programme plan. Pair-drain arms that record while the preceding track is live; confirmed playback of the recorded final track queues its own linked intro first and only then permits the outgoing sign-off plus incoming greeting. Normal placement airs the pair during that final track. Between-tracks placement renders it there but holds it until the first real seam at or after the scheduled boundary. Both lines render before either is published, and the lifecycle remains `queued` until the final line live-edge marker arrives; armed and queued records survive restart. At the real roll, the incoming session inherits its prepared programme state and treats the greeting as its intro. Once claimed, only scheduled talk is suppressed: manual operator speech and listener-request intros remain eligible, while outgoing track links are refused by their existing session stamp. There is no mandatory spacer track. Full invariants and failure directions live in [`show-boundary-handoffs.md`](show-boundary-handoffs.md).
