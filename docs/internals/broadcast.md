# Internals: broadcast, queue and speech timing

Why the queue, transition and speech-timing code is shaped the way it is. The
short invariants are in the root `CLAUDE.md`; read the matching section here
before changing any of them, because most of these paragraphs record a measured
failure and the fix that was chosen over the obvious one.

Covers: the drain/pair-drain path, stem blends, operator skips, spoken-clock
forecasting, air-time stamping, the listener buffer, the listener-count gates,
TTS engine routing, personas, guests, programmes and the policy switches
(voice, clock, token budget).

- **Queue/playback path**: `queue.drainToLiquidsoap()` is the single writer of `next.txt` (+ request-intro `say.txt`); `queue.playJingle()` is the single writer of `jingle-now.txt`; `queue.announce()` is the single writer of scheduled `say.txt`/`intro.txt`. Request-intro WAVs are written ~250ms before the track URI so the 0.5s voice poll picks them up first. Track handoffs poll at 1.0s; voice and manual-jingle handoffs at 0.5s. The manual jingle has a dedicated `request.queue` wrapped around both `dj_queue` and the automatic rotate, so it wins the next **safe** boundary even when a track or automatic jingle is already queued; `source.available` leaves it queued while voice or a bed is live. **Pair-aware drains** (`transitions.pairDrain`, default on, **DJ-mode personas only** — `queue.pairDrainActive()`, `broadcast/drain-policy.ts`): picks are HELD unsent until their successor is queued behind them (the deadline routine in the watcher tick picks it ~120s before the on-air track's effective end, `min(duration, cue_out)`, with a failure-retry cooldown of `DEADLINE_PICK_COOLDOWN_SEC`; hard fallback ~45s drains with intrinsic stamps), which is what lets `applyPairStamps` finally apply the pair-sized `crossSecondsFor` (#749) and lets stem blends insert. A successor of ANY kind releases the hold (a request IS the successor arriving — FIFO is never inverted); unknown clock (boot/recover/untracked auto) or a non-DJ persona drains eagerly, byte-for-byte the old behaviour. **Operator skips commit first (#1300 bug 6)**: because the hold makes an empty `dj_queue` the NORMAL mid-track state, `POST /dj/skip` runs `queue.commitBeforeSkip()` — force-drain plus a bounded wait for the `dj_queue_status` telnet probe to report a RESOLVED request (a sent-but-still-fetching request loses the fallback race just the same) — before the telnet `skip`; on timeout the skip proceeds anyway and the response/booth log say the pick wasn't confirmed, so `auto.m3u` filling the slot is reported, never silent. Policy in `broadcast/skip-policy.ts`, pinned by `scripts/skip-policy.test.ts`; an empty queue keeps the bare-skip path byte-for-byte. **Stem blends** (`transitions.stemBlends`, opt-in; `broadcast/stem-blend.ts` + worker `render_transition`): at a pair drain, a cache-hit-only render mixes the outgoing tail stems × incoming head stems (`state/stems/`, written during analysis when `audio.stemCache` is on) into a clip that airs between them — outgoing cues out at the blend start, the clip carries the INCOMING track's identity (now-playing flips at blend start; `lastSeenKey` dedups the real fire; `subwave_clip="1"` keeps telnet rid helpers honest), incoming cues in past its pre-mixed head. Any miss → plain pair-aware crossfade. **A clip carries no `liq_amplify`** (`subsonic.getClipUri`), so its LEVEL is whatever the render baked in: both sides are gained by `music/loudness.ts` `resolveGainDb` — the same figure the drain stamps on a real track — never by re-deriving from the analyzer's leading-window LUFS, which ignores ReplayGain tags (the default source), the boost cap and the peak headroom cap and is what made rendered seams sit at a different level than the tracks around them (#1240). The render's peak guard ducks the BORROWED loop to fit the ceiling rather than scaling the whole clip, because scaling the clip is scaling the incoming track's own content back out of match. Pinned by `scripts/stem-blend-level.test.ts`. Clips sweep after 1h (clips still queued for an unaired seam are exempt — `queue.pendingClipPaths()`); the stem cache is byte-budget LRU (`audio.stemCacheGb`, 1–1000 GB, edited in Settings → Transitions), and tail stems carry a `tail-meta.json` alignment sidecar (exact decoded tail offset — the render treats stems without it as a miss). **The stem cache backfills** like the CLAP/vocal scopes: `tracks.stems_at` (schema v17) stamps every stem ATTEMPT, `db.needsStemsIds()` is the fourth widening in `runAnalysisPass`, so turning the cache on for an already-analysed library fills in over successive resumable passes instead of needing a `--re-analyze` (which wipes every vector and can't resume). The stamp records the attempt, **not disk presence** — the LRU sweep evicts dirs by design, so a presence-based scope would re-separate every evicted track forever on any library bigger than the budget; a miss at seam time just falls back to a plain crossfade. **The budget gates every stem write, not just the widening (#1257)**: `runAnalysisPass` takes ONE `headroomTracks()` figure per pass and spends it per NET-NEW dir via the pure `stemCache.stemWriteDecision` (a rewrite of an existing dir is free) — ride-along stems used to bypass the cap entirely, which is how a vocal backfill grew a 500 GB budget to 674 GB. Headroom is sized off the cache's own measured average once it holds ≥50 dirs (`estimateTrackBytes`; the fixed 25 MB guess ran ~2x pessimistic in the field), and `sweep()` reports `failedDirs`/`overBudgetBytes` so a sweep that can't evict (permissions on a relocated stems mount) is loud in the booth log, the analyze event log and the doctor instead of returning `{removed: 0}` and reading as "nothing to do". The admin queue header names only the imminent transition after its incoming item is sent (before drain it shows `—`, never a raw proposal), while a `STEM BLEND` text badge stays on the successor row that actually carries `stemSeam`; both are stamped at pair drain, cleared on cancel and definitive rather than predictive. Pinned by `scripts/stem-backfill.test.ts` + `scripts/stem-cache-sweep.test.ts`; the header's label (both sides of the pair, and the sent gate that keeps it a fact rather than a forecast) by `scripts/queue-transition-label.test.ts`.
- **Spoken clocks on pick-attached links (#864 → #1282 → #1314)**: a link is written when the pick is MADE but airs when the pick STARTS, so any time it speaks is a **forecast** — `linkAirDate(showAt)`, i.e. now plus whatever is left of the on-air track. The forecast holds only if the pick reaches Liquidsoap before that track ends; when the round outruns it, `dj_queue` is empty at the seam, `auto.m3u` fills the slot and the pick airs at the END of that filler, with the link still naming the moment the filler STARTED (#1314's ~4-minute undershoot). #1282's removal of a fixed +120s show-attribution overshoot is what exposed it — the two errors had been cancelling. **Two guards, both in `broadcast/queue/pure.ts`, and they must stay paired**: `linkClockAt(showAt, now)` refuses to hand the generator a clock at all below `LINK_CLOCK_MIN_RUNWAY_SEC` (= `DRAIN_DEADLINE_SEC` — `maybeDeadlinePick`'s empty-queue backstop always fires inside that window on any station, whatever its track lengths; measured over 788 rounds, 27.7% ran past 45s of runway but only 0.3% past 120s; accepted flip side: a sub-2-minute-track library never clears the floor even at track start, so its links never speak the clock at all), and `linkClockDrifted` drops the line at `airIntro` when the real seam lands more than `LINK_CLOCK_DRIFT_TOLERANCE_SEC` (90s) from the stamped `item.linkClockAt`. Runway is read from the LIVE clock at generation, never from the `leadSec` `showAt` was built with — on the pool path the pick call has already spent part of it. The stamp records the clock being **offered**, not used: unlike `shouldDropStaleLink`'s `mentionsTrack`, a clock has no reliable string handle ("18:38" / "twenty to seven" / "eighteen thirty-eight"), so don't narrow it with a regex. Note that even a perfect forecast is heard `stream.bufferSeconds` late — see the next bullet.
- **Spoken segments are stamped at AIR, not at handoff (#1382)**: `airVoice()` resolves when the clip's path has been written into `say.txt`/`intro.txt`, which is a 0.5s mixer poll plus the request queue, the 0.8s silent lead-in and the duck ramp before a listener hears a word — so every signal keyed off it (booth log, session turn, `dj.say`/`dj.link`) ran ahead of the stream, the player's own line included. The fix is a fourth marker file in the `jingle-playing.json`/`bed-playing.json` family: `airVoice` mints a `voiceId`, stamps it into the clip's `annotate:` URI as `subwave_voice`, and radio.liq's `voice_queue`/`intro_queue` `on_metadata` hooks echo it into `voice-playing.json` as the clip starts feeding. **Every voice clip is annotated now** (a 0 dB trim used to send the bare path) — that is how the id reaches the mixer, and it is the same channel `subsonic_id`/`subwave_kind` already use; the silent lead-in is deliberately left un-annotated, and that missing id is exactly what makes the hook skip it, so the stamp is the first WORD rather than the start of the duck. Two rules hold the timebase together. **The stamp comes from the marker, never from detection** — `startedAt` is the mixer's own clock, so the 500ms reader cadence delays delivery without moving the timestamp; and **it is live-edge like every other timestamp the controller publishes**, so a LISTENER-facing consumer adds `stream.bufferSeconds` (#1114) — which is why `voice.start` carries `streamBufferSeconds` rather than making a relay go and fetch it. Degrading is load-bearing: a controller upgraded ahead of its broadcast image sees no marker file at all, and `awaitVoiceAir` resolves null **immediately** in that case (never waiting out `VOICE_AIR_TIMEOUT_MS`), which is byte-for-byte the old timing — a 20s stall on every booth-log line would be a far worse regression than the early stamp this fixes. An unmeasured air time is reported as `estimated: true` with the timestamps **absent, not zeroed**. Post-air bookkeeping lives in ONE place, `queue.onSpoken()` — the four sites that air a segment (`announce`, `announceExchange`, `airPendingVoice`, `airIntro`) had each inlined their own and had already drifted into three different payloads — and the payload's `channel` is passed by the caller, never re-derived from `kind`, because a boundary-deferred ident is a say-kind clip that deliberately airs on the INTRO channel. The recent-marker buffer ages on **seenAt, not the marker's own stamp** (a mixer clock running behind would otherwise make every marker look instantly expired). `voice.start`/`voice.end` are the new window events; the `dj.*` pair still fires, unchanged in shape and additive in fields, except for banter, where the aggregate one-per-exchange `dj.say` stays and only `voice.*` goes per line. The session turn carries the same stamp as `meta.airedAt`, which is what finally lets the **player** stop showing a line before it is spoken: `useStationFeed` withholds a voice turn until `airedAt + bufferSeconds`, the identical hold it already applies to the track switch, via the pure `splitAudibleTurns`. That hold fails **open** in both directions — a turn with no stamp, or one whose stamp sits implausibly far ahead (a skewed station clock), renders immediately, because "shown early" is the old behaviour while "never shown" would be a new one. Pinned by `scripts/voice-air-events.test.ts`; the mixer half was verified against a real Liquidsoap 2.4.5 (annotate id → `on_metadata`, lead-in skipped, 0 dB path clean). **`voice.queued` is the third event and the only FORECAST in the set**: stamping at air removed the ~1–2s of accidental early warning firing at handoff used to give, which is what a consumer ducking live audio against the station planned with — and `streamBufferSeconds` doesn't give it back to anyone taking station audio ahead of the Icecast burst (a companion app piping the stream into a phone call sits at the live edge, so `voice.start` and the first word arrive together). It fires from `airVoice`'s new synchronous `onQueued` callback, at the moment the rendered clip joins the voice serialiser and BEFORE any of the waiting, carrying the same `voiceId` plus `estimatedAirInMs`/`expectedAirAt`. Three rules keep it honest. The forecast is **named and flagged as one** (`estimated: true`, and deliberately **no `airedAt`** — a field named for a measurement never carries a guess), it is **never corrected afterwards** (`voice.start` is the measurement, and the ids pair), and **nothing in the controller reads it** — no log line, no session turn, no decision, because a forecast that steers behaviour is a second timebase. The wait is computed by the pure `airInEstimate` from the serialiser's own `_chainFreeAt` and `jingleClearAtMs()`, the two deadlines `airVoice` really sleeps out (they OVERLAP — whichever clears last wins — and a stale jingle marker computes a window in the past, so the floor is 0), plus a fixed `HANDOFF_TO_AIR_MS` head for the 0.5s mixer poll and the 0.8s lead-in. So the head start is **however long the wait happens to be** — many seconds behind another segment, ~1s on an idle chain — which is a head start and not a guarantee; don't add a delay to manufacture one. The four air sites now build ONE `SegmentDesc` read by both `onQueued` and `onSpoken`, for the same reason `onSpoken` was centralised in the first place.
- **Beds under a listener request's intro** (`settings.beds.requestIntros`, #1465, default `true` **within** `beds.enabled`, which is itself `false`): a bed decouples the DJ from the song — Song A → bed (DJ talks) → ramp → Song B — and `bed-policy.bedWanted` originally fired it on ONE question, *would this link outlast the incoming track's intro?* That is damage control for a long script, and it is the wrong question for a request: somebody asked for this track, so its opening bars are theirs whether the DJ needs six seconds or twenty. The two live as a **`BedReason`** (`'link' | 'request'`) rather than as a threshold the caller drops to zero, because a request must also outrank the *measured* budget — including an instrumental's `Infinity`, the one value that short-circuits every other `bedWanted` path, which a `thresholdSec: 0` could never beat. `maybePushBed` discriminates on **`requestedBy`, not `introKind`**: all three request paths (`routes/request.ts` ×2, `dj-agent.runRequest`) push `'dj-speak'`, and so does the studio's own bare push, which carries no script and falls out on the next line anyway. **The channel then follows what the clip plays OVER, not its kind** — the same split the boundary-deferred ident already relies on (#1382). A clip airing on a bed has no song to talk over, so it takes `intro.txt`'s LIGHT duck (`p=0.30`) rather than `say.txt`'s heavy one (`p=0.22`), which would push the bed — an instrumental put there for exactly this purpose — down to a hiss. That routing is what the pre-#1465 comment meant by "bedding them means reworking the duck routing"; nothing in `radio.liq` changed. **And "airing on a bed" is the CALLER's fact, never `item.bedded`**: the flag only means a bed URI reached `next.txt`, and a pushed item is handed over, never playable — an unresolvable URI is dropped in silence and a marker missed by more than `BED_MARKER_FRESH_MS` never fires the event. In both cases the song itself starts and `onTrackStarted` airs the line over ITS opening, which is a song to talk over and takes the heavy duck like any other request intro. Reading the flag instead would hand the one failure case this feature exists to prevent a *lighter* duck than it had pre-#1465, so `onBedStarted` — the only site that has seen the marker — passes `airIntro({overBed: true})`, exactly as `onSpoken`'s `channel` is passed by whoever knows rather than re-derived. Requests never carry `linkPrev` or `linkClockAt`, so neither `shouldDropStaleLink` nor `linkClockDrifted` can drop the line after the bed is committed and leave it airing naked. **The default is a stated behaviour change, not a byte-identical upgrade**: a fresh station is unaffected (beds off), but a station already running beds gains this at restart, which is the trade the sub-switch exists to make reversible. Pinned by `scripts/bed-policy.test.ts` (the reason's four suppression cases), `scripts/beds-request-intros.test.ts` (a COLD-LOAD round trip — the absent-key case IS the upgrade case) and `scripts/voice-channel.test.ts` (the channel rule, whose load-bearing case is the bed that was queued but never aired).
- **The bed's tail is specified, not left over** (`settings.beds.tailSec`, #1485 FR 5c, default 3s): `bedLengthFor` sizes the bed as `entryCross + head + voice + tail + cross`, and the **cross being a term of that sum is the whole fix**. The bed's `liq_cross_duration` starts the next song fading in at `bedSec - crossSec`, so the quiet an operator actually hears between the DJ's last word and the song is `tail - cross` — and while the sum omitted the cross that quiet was a residual of two settings that know nothing about each other, negative at the shipped defaults (2s tail against a 6s bed ramp put the incoming song under the DJ's closing **four seconds**). A bed exists precisely so the DJ is not talking over the song it is introducing, so the feature inverted itself on a default station, and no value of `crossSec` could fix it without also changing how the song comes in. With the cross in the sum the gap is exactly `tailSec` at every ramp length and every script length, which is what makes it a number an operator can reason about; `scripts/bed-policy.test.ts` asserts that independence across the voice × entry-cross × cross matrix rather than asserting the arithmetic, because the arithmetic is what drifted. **This is a stated behaviour change, not a byte-identical upgrade** — the same trade as #1465's `requestIntros`: a fresh station is unaffected (`beds.enabled` is off by default), a station already running beds gets a longer bed with a clean tail at upgrade, and the old talk-up-to-the-vocal overlap is deliberately **unreachable** (that gesture is the un-bedded path, which `bedWanted` returning false already selects; `tailSec: 0` is the hard end of the range, putting the ramp on the last syllable). Two consequences worth knowing: `bedSec` grew by `cross + tail - 2s`, so a short bed file that used to fit can now miss `pickBed`'s length filter and fall back to talking over the track — the booth log reports the tail alongside `bedSec` for exactly that diagnosis — and `bedLengthFor`'s 1s clamp is now structurally unreachable (`crossSec` is a term of `bedSec`), kept as arithmetic insurance and pinned as inert.
- **Listener buffer / live-edge offset (#1114)**: Icecast bursts `settings.stream.bufferSeconds` (default 22s) of already-broadcast audio on connect, so **every listener sits that far behind the live edge for their whole connection**. Two consequences that must stay in sync. (1) `<burst-size>` is a BYTE count, so it's computed **per mount** — `bufferSeconds x that mount's bitrate x 125`, rendered as one `<mount>` block per stream mount by `docker/broadcast-entrypoint.sh` (and the AIO's `render_icecast()`, kept in lockstep), reading `liquidsoap_stream_buffer_seconds.txt` + the per-encoder bitrate files from the state dir; FLAC is VBR so its depth uses a ~900kbps estimate. Never collapse this back to one global figure: the same 528 KB that is 22s of 192k MP3 is ~43s of 96k Opus and ~6s of FLAC (the post-#1114 "Now Spinning runs behind" report). Each mount's `<queue-size>` is 4x its burst (floored at 2 MB) and must stay comfortably above it or clients get evicted the moment they fall behind their own primed buffer. (2) Every timestamp the controller publishes is stamped at the **live edge** (`radio.liq`'s pre-cross `on_metadata`), so listener-facing players shift into listener-time — `useStationFeed` holds the track switch until the audio is actually audible and `trackStartedAt` carries the offset (it can briefly sit in the future; `useElapsed` clamps at 0). The offset the web player applies is the advertised `stream.bufferSeconds` from `/now-playing`, full stop. It used to prefer a per-tab **measurement** (`usePlayer.getListenerLagMs`, `buffered.end − currentTime`) because a byte-sized burst meant a different depth per mount; (1) above fixed that at the source, and the measurement was never the offset anyway. **`buffered.end − currentTime` does NOT measure the listener's distance from the live edge** — it reports only the window the browser has DEMUXED, while Chrome holds the connect burst in an internal cache `buffered` never exposes. Measured directly (tone marker vs `now-playing.json`'s live-edge stamp): true offset **22.5s**, `buffered.end − currentTime` **2.25s**, advertised **22s** — so preferring the measurement flipped every title ~20s early. Don't reintroduce it, and don't use `buffered` to judge cushion health. Operator surfaces (admin dash, MCP) intentionally keep live edge. Changing the depth needs a broadcast **container** bounce, not just a mixer restart — though the telnet `restart` achieves it anyway, since icecast and liquidsoap share a container the entrypoint `wait -n`s on.
- **Listener-count gates (#1256)**: **three** gates fail **OPEN** on an unknown count, and all three read a gated reading, **never the raw `getListenerCount()`** — `listeners.djCallsAllowed()` (all autonomous DJ/LLM work, plus `POST /request`) and the idle-pause monitor (`broadcast/stream-idle.ts`) via `listeners.gatedListenerCount()`, and the analysis quiet gate (`music/analyze-quiet-pure.ts`, whose fail-open runs the OPPOSITE way — an unknown count lets the DSP pass proceed) via `probeListenerCount()`, which returns the gated count from the tagger child's own module state. A failed Icecast status poll is not an observation: the pure `gatedCount` holds the last count actually read from Icecast until failures reach `STALE_STATUS_LIMIT` (4), the same hysteresis the cached `StreamStatus` has carried since #461. On the raw count a **single** `fetchWithTimeout` abort read as "unknown" and the gates opened at once — and because `stream-idle.ts` deliberately forces its own poll every 5s while paused (~120 per 10-minute pause, not the 15s monitor cadence a short manual probe would sample), a sub-1% poll failure rate released nearly every pause ~28s after it engaged: the power save was inactive ~95% of the time it should have been active, and each release also reopened the LLM token gate. Fail-open itself is unchanged — a *sustained* outage still reads null, still resumes, still keeps the DJ on air. Two things keep the failure RATE down rather than absorbing it: the poll is `singleFlight()`-coalesced so the 5s, 15s and on-demand cadences can't race each other into an `AbortError`, and `STATUS_TIMEOUT_MS` is 3000 (not the original 1500) — widening `STALE_STATUS_LIMIT` instead would have slowed #461's offline detection, which is a different contract sharing the same constant. `FIRST_POLL_WAIT_MS` is derived from `STATUS_TIMEOUT_MS`, not written flat, so raising the deadline can't silently un-do the boot await. `presentListeners()` deliberately stays on the RAW count: it fails **CLOSED**, where a blip costs at most one skipped scrobble, which is the safe direction — don't unify it onto the gated reading. `startListenerMonitor()` is awaited before `queue.startWatcher()` so "never polled" can't buy a free agent pick on every restart. Pinned by `scripts/listeners-status.test.ts` and `scripts/stream-idle.test.ts`, which replays 120-poll pause AND live windows through both helpers.
- **On-air prompt memory follows the editorial session (#1479)**: `Queue.djLog` is the station-wide booth/operator ring and intentionally remains whole across show changes; using it as the scripted generators' recap gave a repeated motif up to ten verbatim re-injections for 120 minutes, plus opener fragments with no time cutoff, so an unrelated show inherited the outgoing show's semantic attractor. The prompt source is now the current session's persisted, post-air `segment` turns (`broadcast/prompt-memory.ts` → `session.promptMemory()`), which gives it the lifecycle the session already owns: a genuine `show:<id>` change or the 4h cap hard-rolls to a clean slate, while an autonomous daypart/mood soft shift retains continuity. Do not clear or partition `djLog` to achieve this — the operator history and timing gates still need it. Three explicit bridges obey the same boundary: `buildHandoff` carries previous show/host/mood identity but never its last spoken line, `generateHandoffGreeting` receives the outgoing presenter's identity but never their raw sign-off, and the aired sign-off itself is filtered out of the new session's prompt memory — it is spoken by the outgoing DJ but stored in the session that just started (`runPersonaHandoff` runs after `maybeRoll`), so without that filter the exact line the greeting was denied arrives one segment later. The **mic-pass is the one place the boundary runs backwards**: the sign-off closes the hour that just ended, so `generateSignoff` reads `session.priorPromptMemory()` — an in-memory snapshot the hard roll takes before it archives — rather than the fresh session it is technically speaking inside. Never point it at the live session (empty by construction) and never offer that snapshot to the greeting. Attribution rides along: a segment stamped with a persona other than the session's own is a guest co-host, and the recap prefixes it with the speaker's name exactly as the booth log's `logText` used to, so the host is never handed a guest's words as their own. The stamp itself comes from `queue/pure.ts`'s `exchangeSegment` — the rotation's one attribution builder, named rather than inline precisely because two readers (`windowMessages`, prompt memory) depend on it and it could otherwise drift silently. The listener still hears a real two-voice baton pass; the incoming model is not primed with the outgoing topic. Pinned by `scripts/prompt-memory-boundary.test.ts` (the selection policy, the restart and 4h-cap paths, and the newest-first ordering `getDjRecap`'s cutoff `break` depends on) and `scripts/handoff-memory-wiring.test.ts` (which memory each half of the mic-pass is handed — injected generators, real session and queue).
- **TTS**: callers go through `tts.speak(text, {kind})`, never the engine modules. `tts.js` handles per-kind override + automatic fallback. The Kokoro worker path defaults to `/app/scripts/kokoro_worker.py`; set `KOKORO_WORKER` for non-default layouts. **The rescue chain is a list of voice SLOTS, not engine ids** (`audio/tts-fallback.ts`): the operator's configured fallback (`settings.tts.fallback` — engine AND voice, the TTS analogue of `llm.fallback`, default off), then `defaultEngine` → `piper` → `kokoro`. Only the configured rung carries a `personaTts` override; the hardcoded rungs pass `null`, and that null is load-bearing — a cloud rescue must use the station default's credentials, not the persona provider that just failed. Dedup is by engine, **first-wins**, so a configured fallback naming the same engine as `defaultEngine` keeps its voice rather than reverting to that engine's global default. The same slot serves BOTH triggers (pre-flight reroute in `resolveEngine`, mid-render rescue in `speak`) so there is one answer to "what speaks instead"; `enabled: false` or an absent block is byte-for-byte the pre-fallback behaviour. **A rescue target is engine + provider, not engine (#1345)**: OpenAI, ElevenLabs, Fish and openai-compatible all speak through the one `cloud` dispatcher but are independent failure domains, so `sameTtsTarget` — the ONE place that answers "is this the thing that just failed", never an inline `===` at a call site — compares the provider for `cloud` and the engine alone for everything else. Without it a dead self-hosted Fish server blacklisted a healthy configured ElevenLabs rescue and the station dropped to Piper. An unspecified provider on either side resolves to `settings.tts.cloud.provider`, which is what keeps a station that names no provider anywhere byte-identical. Note the asymmetry with dedup, which stays ENGINE-keyed: a failed provider must not blacklist a healthy sibling, but only ONE cloud rung is ever attempted per rescue — a second is another network round-trip on the same dispatcher while the local floor waits below. Both triggers and both `fellBack` reports (`recordTts`, `describeRouting`) run the same comparison, because an engine-only one reports a fish→ElevenLabs reroute as no fallback at all and hides the misconfiguration Stats and `/debug` exist to show. Pinned by `scripts/tts-fallback.test.ts`.
- **openai-compatible TTS extra params** (`settings.tts.cloud.compatParams`, #1317, default `[]` = off): free-form `{key, value}` body fields merged into the POST to `/audio/speech`, for knobs a self-hosted server defines and the OpenAI API doesn't (Chatterbox's `temperature`/`seed`/`exaggeration`, and whatever the next engine invents — a named setting per server generation doesn't scale). **The merge happens at the fetch layer** (`compatBodyFetch` in `cloud-speech.ts`), not via `providerOptions`: the AI SDK's OpenAI speech model builds a CLOSED body, and its `providerOptions.openai` hatch both validates against a 2-key schema AND (in `@ai-sdk/openai` 4.0.11) drops what it parses through an upstream loop over an empty object — rewriting the serialized body is the only hook that works short of forking the provider. Rules live ONLY in `settings/compat-params.ts`, shared by the validator and the send path so a key that saves is a key that sends; values are stored as text and coerced to JSON types at send time (`0.8` → number, `007` → string, because servers use such values as ids). Four keys are **reserved**: `model`/`voice`/`input` have first-class fields (and an `input` override would mute the segment while still "succeeding"), and `speed` would COMPOUND with the local ffmpeg atempo stretch compat uses instead of server-side speed (#942). The list must stay in `settings.load()`'s explicit `tts.cloud` composition — that block does NOT spread `DEFAULTS`, so a key missing there saves to disk and then silently vanishes on the next restart. Empty keeps the request byte-identical. Pinned by `scripts/compat-tts-params.test.ts`.
- **Daily token budget** (`settings.llm.dailyTokenCap`, default `0` = off): the running per-UTC-day token tally lives in `llm/internal/telemetry/budget.ts` (incremented from the single `record()` chokepoint in `telemetry/log.ts`, the same `ok && usage.total` guard as the lifetime ticker; seeded once at boot from today's `events-*.jsonl` so a restart resumes the count). The pure `budgetMode()` (`core/pure.ts`, unit-pinned) maps `{used, cap, softPct}` → `normal | soft | hard`; **policy lives only in `broadcast/dj-budget.ts`** (the call-site gates), never inline. Tiers: `soft` (≥`budgetSoftPct`%) forces the cheap pool picker and mutes optional segments (links, station IDs, hourly, weather/news/etc.); `hard` (≥cap) makes **no** model call — `runTrackEvent` returns early so Liquidsoap coasts on the LLM-free `auto.m3u` (music never stops). Listener requests stay exempt through the hard cap unless `llm.exemptRequests` is off. Manual `/dj/segment` command routes (the gate-free `runStationId`/`runHourlyCheck`/`runLink` runners) are intentionally **not** budget-gated — an explicit operator action always fires.
- **Station voice switch** (`settings.tts.enabled`, default `true`): `false` makes the station **music only** — no links, idents, hourly checks, skill segments, banter, persona mic-passes, programme beats or spoken request intros. **Policy lives only in `broadcast/voice-policy.ts`** (`autoVoiceAllowed()`, the call-site gates), never inline — same split as `dj-budget.ts`, and the gates sit at the same points those do. The gate is **before generation, not at `speak()`**: gating the dispatcher would still have the LLM write every script and throw it away, so instead `dj-gate.shouldFire()` returns false for every kind (above the frequency ladder — it's not a cadence), `skillsTick`/`ensurePlan`/`maybeRunIntro`/`featureTick`/`outroTick`/`runPersonaHandoff` stand down, `runTrackEvent` forces `wantLink=false` (the agent path's event prompt orders silence, the pool path skips `generateLink`), the request agent's contract drops its intro field (`requestSchema`), and `routes/request.ts` skips `generateIntro` outright. `queue.airIntro()` carries a plain drop as a backstop for items queued before the flip (their WAV render is skipped at drain too; flip back on before the track airs and `airIntro` renders from the script). **Picks keep running** (the stream needs a next track) and **listener requests are still honoured** — track queued, text ack returned, just no spoken intro. Two deliberate non-inclusions: **jingles** are pre-rendered WAVs on Liquidsoap's own rotate, silenced by `jingleRatio: 0` (mixer restart) rather than this switch; and **manual `/dj/segment` triggers still fire**, the same exemption they hold from the frequency gate and the LLM hard cap. Applies live — no restart, no mixer bounce. Absent/non-boolean coerces to ON so an upgrade is byte-identical. Pinned by `scripts/voice-policy.test.ts`. Distinct from per-persona `frequency: 'silent'`, which is per-persona and still speaks request intros, programme beats and manual triggers.
- **Station clock switch** (`settings.djSpeakClock`, #1286, default `true`): `false` keeps the wall clock **off air**, so no time of day in links, idents, hand-overs, ad-libs, banter or programme beats, and the automatic top-of-the-hour time check stands down. **Policy lives only in `broadcast/clock-policy.ts`** (`speakClockAllowed()` / `autoTimeCheckAllowed()`, the call-site gates), never inline: the same split as `voice-policy.ts` and `dj-budget.ts`. It needs its own module because **the clock reaches a model by four independent routes**, and dropping `clock` from the context allowlists closes only the first: the "Local time" line in `llm/internal/prompts/context.ts`; `generateStationId`'s "keep it loose" nudge in `prompts/scripts.ts`, which is pushed whatever the context block holds, so withholding the line alone invites an INVENTED time; the agent path's per-turn clause and `say` schema description (`dj-agent.ts`, `dj-agent/schemas.ts`), which never build context lines at all and, with `llm.pickerAgent` on by default, are where most links come from; and the hourly cron. Two things stay on purpose. **Daypart tags** ("after dark", "weekend", "late night") ride the same context line as the numerals but are atmosphere rather than a clock reading, and `isDark` exists to stop the DJ describing daylight after sunset, so they keep airing under a **"Vibe:"** heading (a "Local time:" carrying no time would read as a bug). And **manual `/dj/segment` triggers stay exempt**, including the operator's "Time check" pad, which is why the hourly gate sits in `dj-gate.shouldFire()` and never inside `generateHourlyTime`: a time check the operator asked for must still be able to say the time. Applies live, no restart and no mixer bounce. Absent or non-boolean coerces to ON so an upgrade is byte-identical. Surfaced on `/debug` as `clock`, beside `voice`. Pinned by `scripts/clock-policy.test.ts`. ⚠️ One-way door: there is no import cycle today because `settings.ts` imports nothing under `llm/internal/prompts`, but if `clock-policy.ts` ever imports anything under `llm/`, the loop closes.
- **DJ persona**: `settings.dj.souls` is an array (1–10); `djSystem()` picks one at random per call. Legacy `dj.soul` string is migrated on load. Emptying the array falls back to seeded `DJ_SOULS`.
- **Station house rules** (`settings.djHouseRules`, #1182, default `''` = off): the one operator block appended to **all three** prompt paths — `renderDjPrompt` (scripted talk), `agentPersonaPreamble` (pick/request/segment agents), and `castHouseRulesBlock` (the multi-voice exchanges), none of which the `djPrompt` template reaches. For path-agnostic correctness rules (TTS control tags, "spell out numbers", locale orthography); the agent-side framing scopes them to spoken fields so ids/reasons stay untouched. The third path was missing until #1420: `generateBanter` and `generateProgrammeExchange` write a whole exchange in one structured call, so they hand-roll a system prompt around a per-call speaker enum instead of going through either of the other two — an operator's rules applied to every link and segment and were silently dropped from every banter line. Their scope wording is owned once (`settings/persona.ts`) and shared by both, since both emit the same `{speaker, text}` shape and a rule like "write numbers out in words" mangles a persona id as readily as a track id. Both system prompts are exported pure (`banterSystem`, `exchangeSystem`) so the test asserts the rendered prompt rather than the existence of a builder. Edited in the System prompt modal on `/admin` Personas; empty keeps every prompt byte-identical. Pinned by `scripts/house-rules.test.ts`.
- **Guest co-hosts** (`shows[].guestPersonaIds`, max 3): while a guest show is on air, standalone segments (station ID, hourly, skill segments, manual link) rotate speakers via `settings.pickOnAirSpeaker()` (host ~60%) — the speaker persona rides through the generator prompt AND `queue.announce({persona, meta:{personaId}})`, so the voice, the prompt seat (`settings.onAirRosterClause`), and the session attribution always agree. **Track picks + their tied intros stay with the HOST** (the pick agent reads `session.windowMessages()` from the host's perspective); gating (frequency, capability ownership) is also host-keyed so who speaks never changes how often the station talks. Co-hosted skills (`SKILL.md` `cohosts: true`) are the other multi-voice consumer: eligibility and cron gating require this same host-plus-guest roster while ownership remains host-keyed; generation runs in `skills/cohosted.ts` against a cast-shaped schema, then `queue.announceExchange()` handles every persona's TTS and attribution. They never use `pickOnAirSpeaker()` and never store an arbitrary cast in the skill. Data gathering follows **`llm.pickerAgent` like every other segment path** — the skill's own tool loop when it is on, a code-driven `fetchSegmentData` + one `djObject` call when it is off. Running the tool loop unconditionally was the bug: pool mode exists for models not trusted with tool loops, so a grounded co-hosted skill could never clear its grounding check there (the tool is reachable only by a model tool call) and stood down every tick after burning a full agent run against `agentTimeoutMs`. The agent leg pins `maxSteps: 2` for the same reason `directorAgent` does (#555). A solo/off-show hour is a **stand-down, not a throw** (`{aired: false, reason}`, the #1412 shape), so Run now, the cron and the programme beat each report it rather than logging a red error. `shows[].banter` (opt-in, needs guests) airs scripted multi-voice exchanges in **windows** opening at :20/:50 (`dj-gate` `'banter'`: quiet=never, moderate=:20 only): one `generateBanter` `djObject` call writes the whole exchange, `queue.announceExchange()` renders every line's WAV first (all-or-nothing), then airs them back-to-back through the serialized `airVoice` chain — no audio mixing anywhere. **The window is the fix for #1419**, and it is why the schedule lives in `broadcast/banter-policy.ts` rather than in a cron string: banter is the only segment gated on a minimum QUIET GAP (`BANTER_MIN_GAP_MS`, 5 min, measured off `queue.getLastTalkBreakAt()` — every standalone break counts, track-tied links don't), and that gap used to be evaluated at exactly two instants an hour. Neither thing that SETS the gap lands on the clock: an ident is scheduled at :15/:45 but boundary-DEFERRED to the next transition (a :15 ident airing at :19:35 is ordinary), and the segment director ticks every 5 minutes with a **zero** floor on `aggressive` (`skills/_agent.ts` `frequencyFloorMs`). Either one lands in the fixed tick's 5-minute shadow, the tick stood down, and the next chance was 30 minutes away — on `moderate`, which owns only the :20 slot, the whole hour. Observed as hours of eligible guest shows airing no banter, so the more talkative the station, the LESS banter it could produce. The slot still opens at :20/:50 (minutes no other wall-clock talker owns — issue #310) but the tick now runs every minute for `BANTER_WINDOW_MINUTES` (10, twice the gap, and short of the :30/:00 ident slots) and fires the first minute the gap is genuinely clear, so a :19:35 ident POSTPONES the exchange to :24:35 instead of cancelling it. Three rules hold it: the gap itself is **untouched** (classifying short idents as not-real-talk is the tempting fix and the wrong one — it lets banter stack right behind one, which is what the gap is for); **one fire per slot**, keyed by `banterSlotKey`, and the claim is taken BEFORE the await, since rendering a multi-voice exchange outlasts a minute and the next tick would otherwise start a concurrent one; and the cron expression is **derived** (`banterCronExpression()`) so the schedule can't drift from the window constants. The frequency ladder stays in `dj-gate.ts` with the other rungs and asks the policy only which slot a minute belongs to — that indirection is what makes :24 read as the :20 slot, so `moderate` keeps its retry without gaining a second exchange. **The stand-down is now LOGGED** with the reason and the numbers (`[banter] stood down at :20 — last standalone talk 25s ago, minimum gap 300s (retrying until :29)`, plus one line when a window closes unfired), once per slot rather than once per tick: the silent `return` is why an hour of starved banter left nothing in the booth log to explain itself. The tick's own state machine is the pure **`banterTickPlan`** — the caller resolves ONE `eligible` flag from the live modules (roster, frequency rung, listeners, budget) and the policy owns the window, the gap and the logging, the same split as `skillCronAllowed` and for the same reason: a rule that reads live settings state itself cannot be pinned. Pinned by `scripts/banter-policy.test.ts`, which replays the reporter's own hour minute by minute (a :19:35 ident → the exchange airs at :25, one stand-down line, not ten) and was mutation-checked against a 1-minute window, a removed log-once guard and a removed slot claim.
- **Programmes** (`shows[].programme`, opt-in; optional `shows[].segmentSkill` pins the feature): the show airs as a produced episode — intro at the top, one feature beat per hour (station-minute :35–:39 window), outro in the final hour's closing minutes (station-minute :55+) (`broadcast/programme.ts`; a single `*/5` cron dispatches on `dueBeat()`'s STATION-zone minute — fixed process-minute crons land mid-show on :30/:45-offset zones like IST; manual `runProgramme*` runners in `scheduler.ts`). Coherence comes from a per-episode **producer plan** (one `generateProgrammePlan` `djObject` call at session start: angle, per-hour feature topics, intro/outro notes) stored ON the session (`session.attachProgramme`); beats are marked aired BEFORE generating (the `markHandoffAired` idempotency pattern) so restarts never double-air. Plan-call failure → `fallback`: beats still air from the raw topic brief. Collision rules: the intro replaces the hourly check in hour one (and on a persona-change boundary the handoff **greeting** doubles as the intro — `episodeAngle` rides into `generateHandoffGreeting`; the standalone intro is skipped); `skillsTick` stands down for the whole programme (`programme.onAir()`); idents/banter keep their slots. The feature runs through the forced segment director (`runCapability(kind, ctx, {brief, persona})`) so it gets real data; a stale/absent kind falls to straight talk (`generateProgrammeFeature`), and so does a capability that **stands down** because its data came back unusable (`aired: false`, `skills/abstain-policy.ts`, #1412) — the beat is mandatory, the invented facts are not. Guest shows open/close with multi-voice exchanges (`generateProgrammeExchange` → `announceExchange`). **Every beat carries `PROGRAMME_GROUNDING_RULE`** (`llm/internal/prompts/programme.ts`, #1301) — one constant appended by all four, never inlined per beat. `PROGRAMME_CONTEXT_FIELDS` shows these prompts NO track fields, so a beat told to be "concrete" about a music topic can only reach for a record it knows from training: a straight-talk feature aired "Smells Like Teen Spirit … blasts in" 16s into an unrelated track. The fix bans the **cue framing** ("playing now / coming up / just heard"), NOT the noun — a grunge feature that can't name *Nevermind* isn't a feature — and asks for certainty as a **separate** half, because real-facts-framed-as-a-cue and invented-credits are independent failure modes. The plan's `featureKindsClause` scopes `kind: null` to topics the host can carry unaided, but that is a frequency lever only: `runFeature` still reaches the ungrounded generator whenever a capability throws at air time. Pure arc helpers (`showSpan`, `planFeature`) live in `broadcast/programme-pure.ts`, pinned by `scripts/programme.test.ts`; the grounding rule + per-beat builders by `scripts/programme-grounding.test.ts`.
